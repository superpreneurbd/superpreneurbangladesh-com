People judge ebooks by their covers. This is not shallow — it is how human brains work. In a crowded marketplace, your cover is your first (and sometimes only) chance to earn a click.

The good news: you do not need to be a designer to have a professional cover. You need to understand what makes covers work, then choose between doing it yourself or hiring help. This guide covers both paths.

What Makes an Ebook Cover Sell

Before discussing tools, understand the principles. Good ebook covers share five traits:

1. Readable at Thumbnail Size

Most buyers first see your cover as a tiny thumbnail — on Amazon search results, on a phone screen, in a Facebook ad. If your title is unreadable at thumbnail size, you lose the click.

Test: Shrink your cover to the size of a postage stamp. Can you still read the title? If not, simplify.

2. One Clear Focal Point

Great covers have one thing for the eye to land on: a bold title, a striking image, or a strong color block. Covers that try to show everything end up communicating nothing.

3. Genre-Appropriate Design

Look at bestselling covers in your category. Business ebooks use clean layouts and bold typography. Fiction uses atmospheric imagery. Self-help uses bright colors and big promises. Your cover should signal "this is the kind of book you are looking for" instantly.

You do not need to copy competitors, but you should speak the same visual language.

4. Professional Typography

The title font matters enormously. Avoid:

  • More than two fonts on one cover
  • Script or decorative fonts for the main title (hard to read)
  • Text that blends into the background (poor contrast)

Do use:

  • Bold, clean sans-serif or strong serif fonts for titles
  • High contrast between text and background
  • Proper spacing — cramped text looks amateur

5. Emotional Resonance

The best covers make the reader feel something: curiosity, hope, ambition, relief. A cover for a freelancing guide might show upward movement or freedom. A cover for a productivity book might feel calm and organized. Match the emotion to the promise.

The DIY Path: Tools and Templates

Canva (Free/Pro)

Canva is the most popular DIY option for good reason. It has hundreds of ebook cover templates, drag-and-drop editing, and exports at the right dimensions.

How to DIY a cover in Canva:

  1. Search "ebook cover" templates
  2. Pick one in your genre (not the prettiest — the most appropriate)
  3. Replace the title, subtitle, and author name
  4. Change colors to match your brand (2–3 colors maximum)
  5. Replace the stock image with something relevant (or remove it for a typography-only cover)
  6. Export as PNG at high resolution

    7. Canva Pro ($120/year) unlocks background removal and premium images. The free version is enough for a first ebook.

    DIY Tips That Make a Real Difference

    • Less is more. A simple cover with great typography beats a busy cover with mediocre design.
    • Use the rule of thirds. Place key elements along imaginary grid lines, not dead center for everything.
    • Leave breathing room. Margins and white space make covers feel premium.
    • Check contrast. Squint at your cover. If the title disappears, fix the contrast.
    • Get feedback. Show your cover to 5–10 people in your target audience. Ask: "What do you think this book is about?" If they cannot tell, redesign.

    When DIY Works Well

    DIY is a smart choice when:

    • This is your first ebook and you are testing the market
    • Your budget is under $50
    • You have basic design sense (you can tell good from bad)
    • Your genre favors simple, typography-driven covers (business, self-help, how-to)

    The Hiring Path: Working With a Designer

    When to Hire

    Hire a designer when:

    • Your ebook is a flagship product (your main revenue driver)
    • You are competing in a crowded category where covers matter enormously
    • DIY attempts look amateurish despite your best effort
    • You value your time more than the $30–$100 a cover costs

    Where to Find Designers

    Fiverr ($15–$100) — Search "ebook cover design" and filter by rating. Look at portfolios, not just prices. The $30–$50 range has many solid designers for nonfiction.

    Upwork ($50–$200) — Better for ongoing relationships. Post your project with clear requirements and review proposals.

    99designs ($199+) — Contest format: multiple designers submit concepts, you pick the winner. More expensive but gives you options.

    Local designers (Bangladesh) — Facebook groups for Bangladeshi designers often have talented people charging ৳1,500–৳5,000 for ebook covers. You get easier communication and local market understanding.

    How to Brief a Designer

    A good brief gets you a good cover. Include:

    1. Book title, subtitle, and author name (exact spelling)
    2. One-paragraph summary of what the book is about
    3. Target audience (who is buying this?)
    4. 3–5 covers you like in your genre (screenshots or links)
    5. Mood/feel in 3 words (e.g., "bold, trustworthy, modern")
    6. Technical specs: dimensions (Amazon KDP wants 2560 x 1600 pixels minimum), format (JPEG/PNG), and whether you need a 3D mockup
    7. What to avoid (colors, styles, clichés you dislike)

      8. Red Flags When Hiring

      • No portfolio or only mockups (not real client work)
      • Unwilling to do revisions (good designers offer 2–3 rounds)
      • Uses copyrighted images without licensing
      • Promises delivery in under 24 hours for complex work (rushed = bad)

      DIY vs Hiring: Honest Comparison

      | Factor | DIY (Canva) | Hiring a Designer |

      |--------|-------------|-------------------|

      | Cost | Free–$120/year | $15–$200 per cover |

      | Time | 2–5 hours of your time | 30 min briefing + review |

      | Quality ceiling | Good | Excellent |

      | Uniqueness | Template-based (others may use similar) | Custom |

      | Best for | First ebooks, testing ideas | Flagship products, competitive niches |

      Cover Mistakes That Kill Sales

      The "everything" cover. Title, subtitle, author photo, testimonial, badge, guarantee seal, and three stock photos. Pick the essentials and cut the rest.

      Unreadable title. Fancy fonts, low contrast, or tiny text. Your title must be readable at thumbnail size. No exceptions.

      Misleading imagery. A cover showing a luxury car for a budgeting ebook creates the wrong expectation. Every element should reinforce your book's actual promise.

      Ignoring the spine and back (for print). If you offer paperback, the spine text and back cover matter too. Do not treat them as afterthoughts.

      Using low-resolution images. Pixelated covers scream amateur. Always work at high resolution (300 DPI for print, at least 1600px wide for digital).

      The 3D Mockup: Worth Adding

      A 3D mockup (your flat cover rendered as a physical-looking book) dramatically improves how your ebook looks in ads, on sales pages, and in social media posts. It makes a digital product feel tangible.

      Free options: Canva has 3D book mockup templates. DIYBookCovers.com offers a free 3D generator.

      Paid options: Most Fiverr cover designers include a 3D mockup for a few dollars extra. Worth it.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      What size should my ebook cover be?

      For Amazon KDP: minimum 1000 x 625 pixels, recommended 2560 x 1600 pixels (1.6:1 ratio). For general use, design at 2560 x 1600 and scale down as needed.

      Can I use stock photos on my cover?

      Yes, if properly licensed. Canva's images are licensed for this use. If hiring a designer, confirm they use licensed images — you are liable if they do not.

      Should my photo be on the cover?

      For nonfiction, author photos on covers are optional. They work well for personal brands and memoir-style books. For how-to guides, a strong typographic cover usually performs better. There is no universal rule — check your genre's conventions.

      How many cover concepts should I test?

      If DIY, create 2–3 variations and get feedback. If hiring, ask for 2–3 initial concepts. Test the finalists with your target audience before committing.

      Do I need a different cover for print vs digital?

      The front cover is the same. Print versions additionally need a spine and back cover.
