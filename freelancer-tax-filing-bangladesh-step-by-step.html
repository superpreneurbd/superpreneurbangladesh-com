Taxes are the topic freelancers avoid until it becomes a problem. Then it becomes a big problem. This guide breaks down freelancer tax filing in Bangladesh into clear steps — what to do, in what order, and when to get professional help.

Important disclaimer: Tax rules change, and this article is educational — not legal or tax advice. For your specific situation, especially as income grows, consult a qualified tax professional or check the National Board of Revenue (NBR) for current rules.

Why Freelancers Need to Think About Taxes Early

Three reasons to get this right from the start:

  1. It is the law. Freelance income is income. Earning online does not exempt you from tax obligations.
  2. Records compound. Every month of clean records makes filing easier. Every month of chaos makes it harder. Start the habit when income is small.
  3. Proof of income unlocks things. Visas, loans, and even some client contracts require documented income. Filed tax returns are the gold standard of proof.

    4. The freelancers who struggle most at tax time are not the ones with complicated income — they are the ones with no records.

    Step 1: Get Your TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number)

    Everything starts with a TIN. Without it, you cannot file a return.

    • Apply online through the NBR's e-TIN registration system. The process is digital and straightforward.
    • You will need your NID and basic personal details.
    • Keep your TIN certificate safe — you will reference this number on every filing.

    Do this now, even if your income is small. Getting a TIN takes little time, and having it ready removes the biggest excuse for not filing later. Many freelancers delay this for years; do not be one of them.

    Step 2: Keep Income Records From Day One

    This is the step that matters most. Before worrying about rates or forms, build the record-keeping habit:

    For international income (Payoneer/Wise):

    • Save every withdrawal confirmation — the amount in foreign currency and the BDT credited to your bank.
    • Keep marketplace earning statements (Fiverr/Upwork provide these).
    • Keep every invoice you send to direct clients, matched with the payment received.

    For local income (bKash/Nagad/bank):

    • Log every payment: date, client, amount, transaction ID.
    • Transfer business earnings to your bank regularly so bank statements reflect your income.

    The minimum viable system: one spreadsheet with columns for date, source, client, amount received (and currency), and transaction reference. Update it when money arrives — not at year end. Fifteen minutes a week beats fifteen painful days in filing season.

    Step 3: Track Deductible Business Expenses

    Your taxable income is generally your revenue minus legitimate business expenses. Track these from the start:

    • Internet and phone bills (the business portion)
    • Software and tools — design software, hosting, domains, AI tools, stock assets
    • Hardware — laptop, phone, accessories used for work (keep receipts)
    • Workspace costs — if you rent a workspace or coworking desk
    • Marketing — ad spend, portfolio website costs
    • Professional fees — what you pay an accountant or tax advisor
    • Training and courses — skills you buy to earn more

    Keep receipts for everything. A photo of each receipt in a dedicated folder works. No receipt, no deduction — that is the practical rule.

    Step 4: Understand What Counts as Income

    For freelancers, taxable income generally includes:

    • Marketplace earnings (Fiverr, Upwork) withdrawn to your accounts
    • Direct client payments (international and local)
    • Digital product sales (ebooks, courses, templates)
    • Affiliate commissions
    • Any other money earned from your freelance work

    Keep personal and business money separate. A dedicated bank account for freelance income makes this trivially easy; a mixed account makes it a forensic exercise.

    Step 5: Know the Filing Timeline

    Bangladesh has an annual tax filing cycle with set deadlines. Missing them can mean penalties.

    • Know your assessment year and the filing deadline for individual taxpayers — check the NBR's current announcements each year, as dates can shift.
    • Do not wait until the last week. The e-filing system gets slow near deadlines, and rushing causes errors.
    • Set a personal deadline two to four weeks before the official one. Future you will be grateful.

    Step 6: File Your Return

    You generally have two routes:

    File yourself through the NBR's online filing system if your income is straightforward — primarily freelance income with clear records and simple expenses. The system guides you through the sections.

    Hire a tax professional (an income tax practitioner) if:

    • Your income comes from many sources or countries
    • You have significant expenses to claim
    • You are unsure about any section of the return
    • The cost of a mistake exceeds the cost of the advisor (it usually does)

    There is no shame in hiring help. A good tax advisor often saves you more than their fee — in correct deductions, avoided penalties, and your own time.

    Step 7: Pay What You Owe, Keep Proof

    After filing, pay any tax due through the official channels and keep the payment receipt with your return copy. Store:

    • The filed return (PDF/printout)
    • Payment receipts
    • Your income spreadsheet for that year
    • Expense receipts

    Keep these for several years. If questions ever arise, organized records are your best defense.

    Special Notes for Bangladeshi Freelancers

    Foreign income documentation: Keep Payoneer/Wise statements showing money received from abroad. These establish the source of your foreign earnings clearly.

    The "10% withholding" question: You may hear that banks deduct tax on foreign remittances. Rules around remittance incentives and withholding change — check the current NBR position for freelance income specifically, or ask a tax professional. Do not rely on hearsay from Facebook groups.

    Growing income = growing complexity. The system that works at ৳30,000/month needs upgrading at ৳300,000/month. Revisit your setup yearly.

    Common Mistakes

    • No TIN, no filing, no records — the triple failure. Fix it in that order.
    • Believing online income is tax-free. It is not.
    • Filing from memory. Human memory is not a bookkeeping system.
    • Mixing personal and business money. Separate accounts solve this.
    • Waiting until the deadline week. Rushed filings have errors; errors have consequences.
    • Taking Facebook-group tax advice as law. Rules change; verify with NBR or a professional.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Do freelancers in Bangladesh need to pay taxes?

    Yes. Freelance income is taxable income. The exact amount depends on current tax slabs, exemptions, and your total income — which is why keeping records and checking current rules (or asking a professional) matters.

    How do I get a TIN in Bangladesh?

    Apply through the NBR's online e-TIN registration with your NID. It is a digital process and the necessary first step before you can file.

    What records do I need to keep?

    Income records (invoices, marketplace statements, Payoneer/Wise confirmations, bKash/Nagad logs) and expense receipts. A simple spreadsheet updated regularly covers the essentials.

    Can I file taxes myself as a freelancer?

    If your income is straightforward and your records are clean, many freelancers file through the NBR's online system themselves. Get professional help when income is complex or you are unsure.

    What happens if I never filed before?

    Do not panic — but do not keep delaying either. Talk to a tax professional about regularizing past years. Starting clean now is always better than starting clean later.

    Are foreign client payments taxed differently?

    The treatment of foreign-earned income can have specific rules (including remittance-related provisions that change over time).
