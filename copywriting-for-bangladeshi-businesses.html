Copywriting means writing words that make people buy. Not fancy words. Not long essays. Just words that turn a reader into a customer. If you are in Bangladesh and want a skill that pays without needing a laptop full of software or a design eye, copywriting is one of the best skills you can learn. Imtiaz Khan writes about this because copywriting sits at the center of every online business he runs.

Why Bangladeshi Businesses Need Copywriters

Walk through any Facebook shop page in Bangladesh and read the product descriptions. Most of them say the same thing: "New arrival. Price: ৳850. Inbox us to order." That is not selling. That is announcing.

Good copy answers the customer's real questions: What is this? Why should I buy it from you? What happens if I do not buy? Most small businesses in Bangladesh — from f-commerce sellers to coaching centers to restaurants — do not have anyone who can write this. They need people who can.

And the demand is growing. Every business that runs Facebook ads, posts on Facebook, or sends WhatsApp messages to customers needs copy. That is almost every business in Bangladesh now.

The AIDA Formula: The Simplest Copy Framework

You do not need a marketing degree to write copy that works. You need one simple formula: AIDA.

A — Attention. Start with a line that makes the reader stop scrolling.

I — Interest. Tell them something interesting about the product or offer.

D — Desire. Make them want it. Talk about the benefit, not just the feature.

A — Action. Tell them exactly what to do next. Click, inbox, call, order.

Here is an example for a hijab seller on Facebook:

  • Attention: "Tired of hijabs that slip off during your office hours?"
  • Interest: "Our premium georgette hijab has a soft inner grip band that stays in place all day."
  • Desire: "Look polished at work without fixing your hijab every hour. Available in 12 office-friendly colors."
  • Action: "Inbox us your address and phone number — cash on delivery across Dhaka."

That short ad does more selling than a full product catalog. Learn AIDA and you already write better than 90 percent of Facebook pages in Bangladesh.

Writing Facebook Ad Copy in Bangla and English

Facebook is where Bangladeshi businesses advertise. So Facebook ad copy is where the money is. Here is what works:

For Bangla copy: Write like you talk. "আপনার ত্বক কি শীতকালে শুষ্ক হয়ে যায়?" beats formal, textbook Bangla every time. Conversational Bangla feels like a friend recommending something. Formal Bangla feels like a textbook.

For English copy: Keep it simple. Your audience reads English as a second language. Short sentences. Common words. "Get clear skin in 14 days" beats "Experience dermatological rejuvenation with our advanced formula."

For Banglish (mixed): This is actually how many Dhaka businesses write naturally, and it works. "Order করুন আজই, delivery in 48 hours." Match how your customer's audience actually talks.

One rule for all languages: one ad, one message, one action. Never try to sell three products or give five instructions in one ad.

Product Description Formulas

Most Bangladeshi online sellers list features. Good copywriters sell benefits. Here is a simple formula you can use for any product:

Name the product. Name the pain. Name the result. Name the next step.

Weak: "Cotton panjabi. Sizes S to XXL. Price ৳1,200."

Strong: "A breathable cotton panjabi for Eid morning prayers — soft enough to wear all day in summer heat. Eid collection is selling fast. Inbox to check your size before it is gone."

Another formula for product pages: Feature → So what? → So what? Take every feature and ask "so what?" until you reach a benefit the customer cares about.

  • Feature: "Made of 100% cotton." So what? "It breathes in summer heat." So what? "You stay comfortable at work all day."
  • The final line is what you write.

Headline Writing Basics

The headline decides whether anyone reads the rest. Five rules:

  1. Be specific. "Save 30% on school bags this week" beats "Great deals on bags."
  2. Talk about the reader, not yourself. "Your kitchen, cleaner in 20 minutes" beats "We sell cleaning products."
  3. Use numbers. They catch the eye. "5 mistakes that kill your Facebook ads" beats "Mistakes in Facebook ads."
  4. Promise one clear benefit. What does the reader get?
  5. Keep it short. Under 10 words is a good target for ad headlines.

    6. Practice this daily: read any newspaper headline or Facebook ad and rewrite it better in your notebook. That is free training.

    Email Copy Basics

    Email marketing is growing in Bangladesh, especially for online courses, ebooks, and B2B services. The basics:

    • Subject line = headline. It decides if the email gets opened. Keep it under 50 characters. Make it curious or useful: "The pricing mistake costing you clients."
    • First line = the hook. If the subject got the click, the first line earns the next ten seconds.
    • One email = one point. Do not cover five topics. One idea, one call to action.
    • Write like a person. "I noticed something in your last campaign" beats "Dear valued customer, we are pleased to inform you."

    How to Practice Daily (Without Clients)

    You do not need clients to build the skill. Do this every day:

    1. Rewrite real ads. Screenshot three Facebook ads you see today. Rewrite each one using AIDA. Keep a folder of before-and-after versions. This is your training and your future portfolio.
    2. Rewrite product pages. Pick a Daraz or local brand product page with a weak description. Write a better one.
    3. Write 200 words a day. Pick any product in your house and write a short ad for it. Speed matters in freelancing; daily reps build it.
    4. Study good copy. Follow Bangladeshi brands that write well. Notice what they do: the opening line, the tone, the call to action.

      5. If you are also building a freelance portfolio from zero, this daily practice gives you spec work to show.

      Building a Copywriting Portfolio from Spec Work

      Clients hire writers they trust, and trust comes from samples. Since beginners have no client work, build spec work — practice pieces written for imaginary clients:

      • Write 5 Facebook ads for imaginary Bangladeshi brands (a restaurant, a coaching center, a clothing shop, a skincare brand, a phone shop).
      • Write 3 product descriptions for real products on Daraz, rewritten by you.
      • Write 1 email sequence of 3 emails for an imaginary online course launch.

      Put them in a Google Doc with a clean cover page, or on a simple free portfolio page. Label them clearly as "spec work." Honesty builds trust.

      Finding Copywriting Clients

      On Upwork: Search for "copywriter," "Facebook ad copy," "product description writer." New freelancers win jobs by writing custom, specific proposals. Never copy-paste a template. Mention the client's actual product in the first line. If you are unsure how to pitch, read our guide on how to write proposals that win clients.

      Locally: This is where many Bangladeshi copywriters miss the easiest path. Walk into (or message) local businesses: coaching centers, restaurants, salons, gyms, clothing shops. Look at their Facebook pages. Show them one rewritten post as a free sample. Many will hire you for monthly Facebook content for 5,000–15,000 BDT per month.

      Facebook groups: Bangladeshi freelancer groups and business groups regularly have posts asking for content writers and copywriters. Answer fast with samples attached.

      Pricing Guidance

      Pricing depends on your level. These are realistic starting ranges:

      • Facebook ad copy: 500–2,000 BDT per ad (local clients); $10–$30 per ad on Upwork/Fiverr when starting.
      • Product descriptions: 300–1,000 BDT each in bulk for local sellers; more on international platforms.
      • Monthly content package: 5,000–20,000 BDT per month for a local business (10–15 Facebook posts + captions).
      • Sales pages / landing pages: 5,000–30,000 BDT as you gain experience; this is where the real money sits.

      Raise your rates every few months as your portfolio and testimonials grow. Never compete on price alone — compete on results. If your ad copy gets a business more orders, you are worth more than the cheapest writer.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Do I need perfect English to be a copywriter?

      No. You need clear English, not perfect English. Most Bangladeshi clients want copy that their customers understand — simple and conversational. Bangla copywriting is also a real market: many f-commerce sellers pay for good Bangla ad copy.

      How long does it take to learn copywriting?

      The basics take 1–2 months of daily practice. Becoming good enough to charge clients takes 3–6 months. Becoming excellent takes years — but you can earn while you improve.

      Is copywriting better than other freelancing skills?

      It pairs well with everything. If you also learn Facebook ads or social media management, copywriting doubles your value. Many successful freelancers in Bangladesh combine writing with another skill. See our guide on [how to start freelancing in Bangladesh](/how-to-start-freelancing-in-bangladesh.html) for the full picture.

      Can I do copywriting with just a phone?

      Yes, for the writing itself. But clients expect documents in Google Docs or Word, and Upwork/Fiverr work is much easier on a laptop. A phone works for learning and practicing.

      What is the difference between content writing and copywriting?

      Content writing informs (blogs, articles). Copywriting sells (ads, product pages, emails). Copywriting usually pays more because it connects directly to revenue. Many writers in Bangladesh start with content writing and move up to copywriting.

      How do I get my first copywriting client?

      Do 2–4 weeks of daily spec work first, so you have samples. Then message 10 local businesses per week with one free rewritten post each. Follow up once.
