Importing from China can give you better margins than buying from local wholesalers. The same product that costs BDT 400 at Islampur might cost you BDT 220 landed from Guangzhou. That margin difference is why so many Bangladeshi sellers eventually look toward China.

But importing is also where beginners lose the most money. Fake suppliers, quality disasters, customs surprises, and shipping delays have wiped out many first-time importers. This guide walks you through the honest, step-by-step process — and just as importantly, tells you when you are NOT ready to import yet.

Step 0: Start with Domestic Reselling First

This is the most important section of this guide, so it comes first.

Do not import as your first business move. Before you spend a single taka in China:

  1. Sell the same (or similar) product sourced locally — from Islampur, Chawkbazar, or New Market.
  2. Prove that customers actually buy it at your price.
  3. Learn your real costs: packaging, delivery, returns, COD failures.

    4. Why? Because importing locks your money up for 30–60 days (production + shipping + customs). If the product does not sell, you are stuck with a container of dead stock instead of a small local batch you can clear quickly. Domestic reselling is your low-cost market research. Our guide on selling on Facebook in Bangladesh covers exactly how to do this.

    Import when you have a proven product and the capital to scale it — not to discover whether it sells.

    Step 1: Find Products Worth Importing

    Good import products share these traits:

    • Small and light — shipping cost is driven by volume and weight. Phone accessories beat furniture.
    • Not fragile — glass and ceramics break in transit; claims are painful.
    • Proven demand — you already sell it locally (see Step 0), or it sells strongly on Facebook and in markets.
    • Hard to find locally — if every wholesaler in Dhaka already stocks it cheap, your margin edge disappears.
    • Not branded — importing counterfeit branded goods (fake Nike, fake Apple accessories) risks customs seizure and legal trouble. Sell unbranded or your own brand.

    Avoid electronics with batteries (shipping restrictions), food items (health certifications), and anything requiring special government approval until you have experience.

    Step 2: Find Suppliers on Alibaba and 1688

    Alibaba (alibaba.com) is the main platform for international buyers. It is in English, supports Trade Assurance (payment protection), and most suppliers are used to working with foreign buyers.

    1688 (1688.com) is Alibaba's domestic Chinese platform. Prices are often 10–30% lower than Alibaba for the same products, but the site is in Chinese and most sellers do not ship internationally or speak English. You typically need a sourcing agent (Step 3) to buy from 1688.

    Vetting suppliers on Alibaba:

    • Prefer suppliers with 3+ years on the platform, verified badges, and high transaction volumes.
    • Check reviews and, more importantly, the number of repeat buyers.
    • Message 5–10 suppliers for the same product. Compare not just price but responsiveness, MOQ (minimum order quantity), and willingness to send samples.
    • Always order samples first. A BDT 2,000–5,000 sample order (including express shipping) can save you from a BDT 2 lakh mistake.
    • Use Trade Assurance for payment protection on your first orders. Never wire money directly to a personal bank account.

    Red flags: prices far below every other supplier, refusal to send samples, pressure to pay outside the platform, no business license when asked, and brand-new accounts with zero transaction history.

    Step 3: Consider a Sourcing Agent

    A sourcing agent is a person or company in China who finds suppliers, checks quality, consolidates your goods, and arranges shipping. For beginners, a good agent is worth every penny.

    What agents typically do:

    • Source products from 1688 at domestic prices
    • Inspect goods before shipping (quality control)
    • Combine products from multiple suppliers into one shipment
    • Handle export paperwork on the China side
    • Arrange freight forwarding to Bangladesh

    What they cost: Usually a 3–10% commission on the order value, or a flat fee per order. Some agents work on shipping margins instead.

    How to find one: Ask in Bangladeshi importer Facebook groups for recommendations — referrals beat Google searches here. Start with a small test order to evaluate an agent before trusting them with large money. A good agent communicates clearly, sends inspection photos/videos without being asked, and is transparent about every cost.

    If your order is simple (one product, one supplier, small quantity), you can manage without an agent using Alibaba + a freight forwarder. For 1688 orders or multi-supplier consolidations, an agent is strongly recommended.

    Step 4: Understand Shipping — Air vs. Sea

    This is where beginners get confused, so here are the basics:

    Air freight:

    • Fast: 7–15 days to Dhaka
    • Expensive: charged by weight (per kg)
    • Best for: samples, small test orders, urgent restocks, lightweight high-value goods

    Sea freight:

    • Slow: 25–45 days to Chattogram port
    • Cheap per unit for larger volumes
    • Best for: bulk orders once a product is proven

    CBM basics: Sea freight is priced by CBM (cubic meter) — the volume your goods occupy. One CBM = 1m × 1m × 1m of cargo. A freight forwarder quotes you a price per CBM. Small, dense products (like phone cases) fit far more units per CBM than bulky ones (like storage boxes). This is another reason small, light products win for beginners.

    Freight forwarders handle the shipping logistics — booking cargo space, customs clearance coordination, and delivery to your warehouse. Many Bangladeshi importers use Dhaka/Chattogram-based forwarders who specialize in China–Bangladesh routes and quote all-in rates (shipping + customs handling). Get quotes from 3 forwarders before your first shipment; prices and service vary widely.

    Key cost to remember: Always calculate your landed cost — product cost + agent commission + international shipping + customs duty + local transport to your location. The supplier's unit price is never the full story.

    Step 5: Customs and Import Paperwork Basics

    To import commercially into Bangladesh, you generally need:

    • Trade license — from your city corporation or union parishad
    • IRC (Import Registration Certificate) — issued by the Chief Controller of Imports and Exports (CCI&E); this is the core document that makes you a legal importer
    • TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number) — for tax purposes
    • Bank account — for L/C (letter of credit) or other permitted payment methods
    • Membership of a chamber of commerce or trade body (required for IRC in most cases)

    Customs duty varies by product category (HS code). Rates can range from moderate to very high depending on what you import. Before committing to a product, ask your freight forwarder or a C&F (clearing and forwarding) agent for the approximate duty rate on that HS code. A product with great margins in China can become unprofitable after duty — check this BEFORE ordering, not after the goods arrive at the port.

    Honest note: Many small sellers start by importing small quantities through informal channels or consolidators who handle everything for a flat per-kg rate. This is common, but it has limits — no proper documentation means no legal recourse if goods are lost, and scaling eventually requires going formal. Plan your path to proper documentation as you grow.

    This is general information, not legal advice. Import rules change — verify current requirements with a C&F agent or the CCI&E before you commit money.

    Step 6: Realistic Minimum Budgets

    Here is what importing honestly costs at different levels:

    Test order (air freight, small quantity):

    • Samples + small batch: BDT 30,000–80,000
    • Good for: validating quality and testing a product you already sell locally

    First real order (sea freight, one CBM or less):

    • Goods + shipping + customs: BDT 1.5–3.5 lakh
    • Good for: a proven product with steady local sales

    Serious import run (multiple CBMs):

    • BDT 5 lakh and up
    • Only for: products with months of proven sales data

    Notice the pattern: each level up requires proof from the level below. The importers who lose money are the ones who skip straight to a big sea shipment on an unproven product.

    Step 7: Common Scams and How to Avoid Them

    Fake suppliers: They take your payment and disappear. Defense: Trade Assurance, verified suppliers with history, never pay to personal accounts.

    Bait-and-switch quality: The sample is perfect; the bulk order is garbage. Defense: pre-shipment inspection (your agent or a third-party inspection service checks goods before they ship — costs around $100–300 and is worth it on larger orders).

    Hidden costs: A forwarder quotes a low rate, then adds "unexpected" charges at the port. Defense: get written all-in quotes, ask specifically "what could add to this cost?", and use forwarders recommended by other importers.

    Counterfeit risk: A supplier offers "OEM" branded products at impossible prices. Defense: do not import branded goods unless you are an authorized distributor. Customs can seize them, and you lose everything.

    The golden rule: If a deal looks too good to be true, it is. Compare multiple quotes for everything — suppliers, agents, forwarders. Outliers (much cheaper than everyone else) are warnings, not bargains.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How much money do I need to start importing from China to Bangladesh?

    A small air-freight test order runs BDT 30,000–80,000. A first real sea-freight order typically needs BDT 1.5–3.5 lakh all-in (goods, shipping, customs). Start small and scale only with proven sales.

    Is Alibaba safe for Bangladeshi buyers?

    Alibaba itself is a legitimate platform, but individual suppliers vary. Use Trade Assurance for payment protection, stick to verified suppliers with multi-year history, always order samples first, and never wire money to personal bank accounts. Most scams target buyers who skip these steps.

    What is 1688 and should I use it instead of Alibaba?

    1688 is Alibaba's Chinese domestic marketplace with prices often 10–30% lower. But it is in Chinese, sellers rarely speak English or ship internationally. You need a sourcing agent to buy from 1688. Use it once you have an agent and order volume that justifies the extra step.

    Do I need an import license to import from China?

    For commercial importing, yes — you need a trade license and an IRC (Import Registration Certificate) from the CCI&E, plus a TIN. Many small sellers start with small quantities through consolidators, but formal documentation becomes necessary as you scale. Verify current requirements with a C&F agent.

    How long does shipping from China to Bangladesh take?

    Air freight: 7–15 days. Sea freight: 25–45 days to Chattogram port, plus customs clearance and local transport time. Always add buffer — plan for delays, especially around Chinese New Year when factories shut down for weeks.

    What products should beginners avoid importing?

    Avoid fragile items, electronics with batteries, food products, branded/counterfeit goods, and anything needing special government approval. Start with small, light, unbranded, non-fragile products with proven local demand. ## The Bottom Line Importing from China is a margin game, and margins reward the prepared.
