Nagad is the second pillar of mobile payments in Bangladesh. If you sell online — digital products, courses, Facebook shop items, or services — many of your customers will want to pay with Nagad. Setting up a proper merchant account (instead of improvising with a personal number) makes you look professional and keeps your money organized.

This guide covers what a Nagad merchant account is, how to set one up, and how to run it day to day.

Why a Merchant Account Instead of a Personal Number?

The same logic that applies to bKash for business applies here:

  • Higher transaction limits than personal accounts — essential when sales pick up.
  • Business payment features like merchant collection tools and transaction reports.
  • Cleaner records for bookkeeping and taxes — business money stays identifiable.
  • Customer trust — paying a registered merchant feels safer than sending money to a stranger's personal number.

If you only take a payment now and then, a personal Nagad account works temporarily. The moment payments become regular, get the merchant setup.

What You Typically Need to Register

Business onboarding requirements change over time, so always check Nagad's official business/merchant page for the current list. In general, expect to provide:

  1. Your NID — valid, with a clear photo.
  2. Business documentation — such as a trade license. Requirements vary by business type and size.
  3. A mobile number for the merchant account — ideally a dedicated business SIM, not your personal daily number.
  4. Business details — business name, address, and what you sell.

    5. Prepare before you apply. Having documents ready in clear, well-lit photos avoids the back-and-forth that delays most applications. Name spellings must be consistent across every document.

    The Registration Process

    While exact steps evolve, the general flow is:

    1. Start the merchant registration through Nagad's business channel (website, app business section, or authorized agent — use official channels only).
    2. Submit your documents — NID, business papers, and the requested details.
    3. Verification — Nagad reviews your application. This can take several business days.
    4. Activation — once approved, you receive your merchant number/wallet and access to merchant features.
    5. Test it — receive a small test payment from your own personal account before announcing the number to customers.

      6. Use official channels only. Never hand your NID or business documents to a random "agent" on Facebook who promises fast-track merchant approval. Account fraud is real, and your documents are valuable.

      Collecting Payments: How It Works Day to Day

      Give customers clear instructions

      Every payment instruction you send should include:

      • The exact merchant number or payment method
      • The exact amount
      • What reference to include (order number, phone number, or name)
      • A request to share the transaction ID after paying

      Example: "Please pay ৳1,200 to our Nagad merchant 01XXXXXXXXX via the Payment option. After paying, reply with your TrxID and order number."

      Verify every payment yourself

      Just like with bKash: a payment is confirmed only when you see it in your own Nagad transaction history. Customer screenshots are not proof — they can be edited. The TrxID must appear in your records.

      Reconcile regularly

      At the end of each day (or each sales batch), match payments received against orders placed. Unmatched payments usually mean:

      • The customer forgot to include their order reference
      • The customer paid the wrong amount
      • The customer paid to the wrong number

      A simple spreadsheet — date, customer, amount, TrxID, order status — prevents these from turning into lost money or angry customers.

      QR and Digital Collection

      If Nagad offers QR-based merchant collection in your setup, use it. QR codes remove the two biggest sources of payment errors: wrong numbers and wrong amounts. Display the QR on your Facebook page, website checkout, invoices, and anywhere customers pay.

      For online sellers, a fixed payment flow beats improvisation every time. The fewer decisions a customer has to make at checkout, the fewer payments go wrong.

      Fees and Pricing: Be Transparent

      Mobile money has costs — cash-out fees exist on someone's side. Decide your policy before your first sale:

      • Absorb the cost into your price (smoothest customer experience)
      • State clearly that the customer covers it
      • Split it somehow — but keep it simple

      Whatever you choose, write it down where customers see it before they pay. Surprise deductions after payment destroy trust.

      Record-Keeping for Business and Taxes

      A merchant account gives you better transaction records than a personal number — use them:

      1. Download or note your statements regularly. Do not rely on the app's history view alone for old transactions.
      2. Keep your sales log. The spreadsheet habit from day one: date, customer, product, amount, TrxID.
      3. Separate business and personal money. A dedicated merchant number plus regular transfers to your bank account keeps things clean.
      4. Save everything for tax time. Proper records are what make freelancer tax filing possible instead of painful.

        5. Nagad + bKash: Should You Offer Both?

        Yes — if you can manage it. Customers have strong preferences, and some only use one service. Offering both removes a reason for a customer to abandon their purchase. The operational cost is one extra reconciliation step per day, which your spreadsheet already handles.

        Many Bangladeshi online sellers run both bKash and Nagad merchant collection side by side. Start with whichever your customers ask for most, add the second when volume justifies it.

        Common Mistakes

        • Registering with inconsistent names. Every document must show the same name spelling. Fix mismatches before applying.
        • Using unofficial agents. Only use Nagad's official registration channels.
        • Skipping the test payment. Always test with a small amount before going live.
        • No payment instructions. "Just send to this number" causes wrong amounts, missing references, and reconciliation chaos.
        • Trusting screenshots. Verify in your own transaction history. Always.
        • Mixing personal and business money. A dedicated merchant number costs little and saves enormous headaches.

        Frequently Asked Questions

        Is a Nagad merchant account free to open?

        Opening is typically free; transaction and cash-out fees apply when money moves. Check Nagad's current merchant fee schedule before you start so you can price accordingly.

        Can I run a business on a personal Nagad account?

        Temporarily, for very small volumes — but limits, mixed finances, and unprofessional payment experience become problems fast. Get the merchant setup once sales are regular.

        How long does merchant approval take?

        It varies — typically several business days if your documents are complete and clear. Incomplete or blurry documents are the main cause of delays.

        What if a customer sends money to the wrong number?

        If they used manual transfer to a wrong number, recovery is difficult — this is why QR codes and payment links are better. For merchant-number payments, the structured flow reduces this risk significantly.

        Do I need a trade license for a Nagad merchant account?

        Business documentation requirements depend on the account type and current policy. Check Nagad's official merchant registration page for the exact current requirements for your situation.

        Should I offer both Nagad and bKash?

        If you can handle the extra reconciliation, yes. Customers have strong preferences, and offering both removes friction at checkout.
