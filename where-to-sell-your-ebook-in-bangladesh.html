You wrote the ebook. You formatted it. You designed the cover. Now comes the question every author asks: where do I actually sell this thing?

For Bangladeshi authors, the answer is more complicated than for Americans or Europeans. Payment processing, international transfers, and local buying habits all affect which platform works best. This guide compares every realistic option.

Option 1: Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP)

What it is: Amazon's self-publishing platform. The largest ebook marketplace in the world.

Pros:

  • Massive built-in audience (millions of Kindle readers)
  • Free to publish, no upfront costs
  • 70% royalty on ebooks priced $2.99-$9.99
  • Kindle Unlimited can generate extra income from page reads
  • Professional credibility ("published on Amazon")

Cons:

  • Getting paid is complicated from Bangladesh. Amazon pays via bank transfer or check. Bangladeshi authors typically need a Payoneer account to receive payments, which adds a step and fees.
  • High competition - millions of ebooks
  • Amazon controls pricing promotions and can change terms
  • 35% royalty outside the $2.99-$9.99 range

Payment reality check: Amazon pays 60 days after the end of the sales month. If you earn in January, you get paid end of March. Through Payoneer, expect to lose a few percent in transfer and conversion fees. It works, but it is slow.

Best for: Authors targeting international readers, especially in English.

Option 2: Gumroad

What it is: A platform for selling digital products directly to your audience. You get a product page, checkout, and delivery.

Pros:

  • Simple setup (product page live in 30 minutes)
  • You control pricing, discounts, and bundling
  • Direct relationship with buyers (you get their emails)
  • Supports PDF, EPUB, and other digital formats
  • Good for selling to your existing audience

Cons:

  • No built-in traffic. Gumroad does not bring buyers - you bring them.
  • Fees: 10% flat fee plus payment processing on the free plan
  • Payment to Bangladeshi sellers goes through PayPal or bank transfer, which has its own complications
  • Less discovery than Amazon

Best for: Authors with an existing audience (YouTube subscribers, email list, social media followers) who want maximum control.

Option 3: Your Own Website

What it is: Selling directly through your own site using a payment plugin or simple checkout.

Pros:

  • Zero platform fees (beyond payment processing)
  • Full control over branding, pricing, and customer data
  • Highest profit margin per sale
  • Builds your brand, not someone else's platform

Cons:

  • You handle everything: product delivery, customer support, refunds
  • Need to set up payment processing (challenging in Bangladesh)
  • No built-in trust - buyers must trust YOU, not a platform
  • Requires technical setup (WordPress + WooCommerce, or similar)

Payment options for Bangladeshi websites:

  • bKash/Nagad merchant accounts - work for local buyers, manual delivery
  • SSLCommerz - local payment gateway supporting cards, mobile banking, and net banking
  • PayPal - not fully functional for receiving in Bangladesh
  • Stripe - not available for Bangladeshi businesses directly

Best for: Established authors with traffic and technical comfort. Highest long-term value.

Option 4: Local Bangladeshi Platforms

Several local platforms cater specifically to Bangladeshi digital product sellers.

What to look for:

  • bKash/Nagad payment integration (critical for local buyers)
  • Reasonable commission rates (compare carefully - some take 20-30%)
  • Bangla language support
  • Local customer support

Pros:

  • Buyers pay with familiar methods (bKash is ubiquitous)
  • No international transfer headaches
  • Marketing in Bangla resonates locally

Cons:

  • Smaller audiences than global platforms
  • Platform policies and reliability vary - research before committing
  • May lack features like affiliate programs or advanced analytics

Best for: Bangla-language ebooks targeting Bangladeshi readers who prefer mobile banking payments.

Option 5: Social Media Direct Sales

What it is: Selling through Facebook pages, groups, WhatsApp, or Instagram DMs. Buyer pays via bKash/Nagad, you send the file manually or via automated link.

Pros:

  • Zero platform fees
  • Works with how Bangladeshis actually buy (conversational commerce)
  • No technical setup needed
  • Personal touch builds trust

Cons:

  • Does not scale - every sale needs your involvement (unless automated)
  • Looks less professional than a proper checkout
  • Hard to track analytics
  • Refund and dispute handling is messy

How to make it work at small scale:

  1. Create a simple order form (Google Form works)
  2. Buyer fills the form and sends bKash payment to your merchant number
  3. You verify payment and send the download link
  4. As volume grows, automate with email tools or a simple WooCommerce setup

    5. Best for: First 50-100 sales while you validate demand. Almost every Bangladeshi digital product seller starts here.

    The Smart Strategy: Start Narrow, Expand Later

    Do not try to be everywhere on day one. Here is a practical sequence:

    Phase 1 (first sales): Sell through social media direct + a simple order form. Validate that people actually want your ebook. Target: 50 sales.

    Phase 2 (systemize): Move to Gumroad or your own website with SSLCommerz/bKash integration. Automate delivery. Target: 200 sales.

    Phase 3 (scale): Add Amazon KDP for international reach. Consider local platforms for wider Bangladeshi distribution. Your own website remains the home base.

    This sequence matches effort to proof. You do not need a perfect multi-platform setup before your first sale.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can I sell on multiple platforms at once?

    Yes, with one exception: if you enroll in KDP Select (Kindle Unlimited), Amazon requires exclusivity for the ebook version. You can still sell print elsewhere. Without KDP Select, sell everywhere.

    How do I handle VAT and taxes on ebook sales?

    Tax rules for digital products vary. In Bangladesh, consult a tax professional about whether your ebook income counts as business income and what records to keep. Keep every payment receipt from day one - this makes tax filing much easier later.

    What if a platform shuts down or bans me?

    This is why owning your customer list matters. Always collect buyer emails (with permission). If a platform disappears, you can still reach your audience. Never build your entire business on rented land.

    Should I offer my ebook for free anywhere?

    Free copies for reviewers: yes. Free as a permanent strategy: only if it feeds a larger funnel (email list leading to a paid product). A permanently free ebook with no upsell is a missed opportunity.
