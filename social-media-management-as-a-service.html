A social media manager runs a business's social media pages: you create the posts, reply to comments and messages, grow followers, and report results — while the owner focuses on their business.

This is one of the most beginner-friendly services you can sell in Bangladesh: no degree, no big investment, no office. You need a phone or laptop, an internet connection, and the skills in this guide.

I am Imtiaz Khan, a Dhaka-based digital marketer. Here is exactly what social media managers do, what to charge, and how to get your first paying client.

What Social Media Managers Actually Do Day-to-Day

The daily work is practical and repeatable. Here is a typical day managing two or three client pages:

Morning — check and respond. Reply to new comments and inbox messages for each client page. Most replies take under a minute, and this is the highest-value part of the job — customers judge businesses by response speed.

Midday — create and schedule. Design the week's posts in Canva — product photos, offers, tips, behind-the-scenes. Write short captions in the brand's voice and schedule everything in Meta Business Suite.

Weekly — review numbers. Check each page: which posts got the most reach and engagement? Plan next week around what worked.

Monthly — report to the client. Send a one-page report: followers gained, best posts, messages handled, next month's plan. This report keeps clients paying.

Respond fast, post consistently, show the numbers. Do this well and clients stay for years.

Why Bangladeshi Businesses Hire Social Media Managers

The demand is simple: business owners have no time.

A clothing shop owner in Dhaka spends her day on stock, staff, and customers. She knows her Facebook page drives sales, but daily posting, replying to hundreds of comments, and designing graphics is a second full-time job she cannot do.

Restaurants, coaching centers, salons, agents, online sellers — they all need someone reliable who "just handles it." That someone can be you.

Most owners are also bad at content: blurry photos, three silent weeks, then five posts in one day. A manager brings consistency — and consistency turns a dead page into a selling machine clients can see in their sales.

Skills You Need (And How to Learn Each One)

You need four core skills, all learnable in 30 to 60 days of practice.

1. Content creation. Writing short captions and making simple graphics. Practice by running your own page for a month — post daily and try different styles. Your page is your training ground and portfolio.

2. Scheduling and planning. Knowing what to post and when. Learn Meta Business Suite (free) to schedule posts in advance, and plan one week of content every Sunday. Batching saves hours.

3. Basic design. You do not need to be a graphic designer. You need Canva (free) and clean layouts: one clear photo, short text, brand colors. Study pages you admire and copy their structure.

4. Customer communication. Replying to comments and inbox messages politely and fast. Write templates for common questions (price? delivery? location?) so you answer in seconds.

Bonus skill: basic video. Short vertical videos (Reels) get far more reach than photos now. Learn to shoot and trim phone videos in CapCut (free) — this one skill can justify higher prices.

The Tools You Need (Most Are Free)

  • Meta Business Suite (free) — Schedule posts, reply to messages in one inbox, see insights. Your main dashboard.
  • Canva (free plan) — Design posts, stories, and covers. The free plan is enough to start.
  • CapCut (free) — Edit short videos on your phone: trim, captions, music.
  • Google Sheets (free) — Your content calendar: one row per post with date, platform, caption, and status.
  • A scheduling backup — Meta Business Suite covers Facebook and Instagram. For TikTok or LinkedIn, use the native schedulers or Buffer's free tier.

Total software cost to start: zero taka. Your investment is time and practice.

How to Package the Service

Clients do not buy "social media management." They buy a clear package. Make yours simple with three tiers:

Starter — for small pages. 8 posts per month, 1 platform (Facebook), weekday comment and inbox replies, monthly report. Your entry offer.

Growth — the most popular. 12 to 16 posts per month, 2 platforms (Facebook + Instagram), daily replies, 4 short videos, monthly report with recommendations.

Premium — for serious sellers. 20+ posts per month, 3 platforms, priority replies within 2 hours, 8 short videos, monthly strategy call.

Most clients pick the middle tier. Always include the monthly report — it makes your work visible. Invisible work gets cancelled.

One rule: put everything in writing before you start — monthly deliverables, what the client must provide (photos, product info), payment terms, and a 30-day cancellation notice. This prevents 90% of client problems. Our guide on building a freelance portfolio from zero shows how to create proof that wins clients.

Pricing Guidance in BDT

Pricing is where beginners freeze. Here is practical guidance — treat these as starting points for someone new, not fixed market rates. Raise your prices as your results and demand grow.

  • Starter package: BDT 5,000 to 8,000 per month. Fair when you are building your portfolio.
  • Growth package: BDT 10,000 to 15,000 per month. The sweet spot for most small businesses once you have 2 to 3 months of proof.
  • Premium package: BDT 20,000 to 30,000+ per month. For established sellers who need daily content and fast replies.

Retainers give you stable income and the client a committed partner. Per-post pricing turns you into a task worker clients haggle with. Sell the monthly package.

Start at the lower end for your first one or two clients. After 3 months of good results, raise new-client prices by 20 to 30%, and give existing clients 30 days' notice tied to results. Clients pay for outcomes, not effort.

Finding Your First Clients

You do not need ads or a website to get client one. You need proof and outreach.

Step 1: Build proof on your own page (30 to 60 days). Run your own Facebook page or Instagram account seriously. Post consistently, grow it, screenshot everything. Clients ask "show me pages you manage" — your own page is the answer.

Step 2: List 20 local businesses with weak pages. Walk through your area or scroll Facebook: restaurants with blurry photos, shops posting once a month, coaching centers ignoring comments. Their problem is visible — they are warm prospects.

Step 3: Send a simple message. Not a sales pitch — a specific observation. Example: "I noticed your page has great products but posts only twice a month, and several comments have no reply. I manage social media for small businesses — 12 posts a month plus daily replies. Can I send you a free one-week content plan?" Short, specific, and offering value first.

Step 4: Offer a one-month trial at starter price. One month is enough to show results — better posts, faster replies, a clean report. Results turn trials into long-term retainers.

Facebook groups for business owners, referrals from your first client, and your own network — tell everyone what you do. The first client is the hardest; the second comes from the first client's results.

Reporting Results Simply

Your monthly report is your retention tool — one page, four sections:

  1. Growth: followers at start vs end of month. One line, two numbers.
  2. Best content: screenshots of the top 3 posts with reach and engagement numbers.
  3. Conversations: how many comments and messages you replied to, and your average response time.
  4. Next month: 3 bullets on what you will change based on the data.

    5. Send it on the 1st of every month, plus one sentence tying your work to their business: "Price inquiries rose in the last two weeks, matching our best product posts."

    Clients read short reports that show progress. A client who sees monthly progress does not cancel.

    Growing From One Client to Many

    One client is a side income. Five clients at growth prices is a real business. Here is how to scale:

    Build templates. Before taking client three: captions, Canva designs per industry, reply templates, a standard report format. Templates turn 10 hours of work into 5.

    When you have more inquiries than time, raise prices for new clients. That is the market telling you that you are underpriced.

    Specialists charge more and get referrals faster — every clothing brand owner knows other clothing brand owners.

    At 5 to 6 clients you will hit your time limit. Hire a part-time designer or assistant — even a skilled student. You keep relationships and strategy; they handle production. That is how a freelancer becomes an agency owner.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What does a social media manager do every day?

    Reply to comments and messages, create and schedule posts, check performance numbers. For two to three clients, this takes 2 to 4 focused hours a day with templates.

    Do I need a degree or certificate to become a social media manager?

    No. Clients hire based on proof, not paper. A well-run page with real engagement convinces clients faster than any certificate.

    How much can a beginner charge in Bangladesh?

    Beginners typically start around BDT 5,000 to 8,000 per month for 8 posts plus comment replies. With 3 months of proven results, most raise to BDT 10,000 to 15,000. Price on value and proof, not hope.

    How do I get my first client with no experience?

    Run your own page for 30 to 60 days as your portfolio, then message 20 local businesses with weak pages and one specific fix you would make. Offer a one-month trial. Results turn into retainers.

    Should I manage pages for free to build a portfolio?

    One short free trial (one to two weeks) for your very first client is acceptable. Never work months for free. Your own page plus one trial is enough proof to start charging.

    When should I raise my prices?

    When you have three months of good results and more inquiries than time.
