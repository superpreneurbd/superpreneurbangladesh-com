You finished your manuscript. Congratulations — that is the hard part. But right now it probably looks like a long Word document: inconsistent headings, random spacing, maybe some bold text scattered around. Before anyone will pay for it, it needs to look like a real book.

Good news: professional ebook formatting is a learnable skill, and you can do it yourself for free. This guide walks through the entire process, from raw manuscript to a polished file ready to sell.

Why Formatting Matters More Than You Think

Readers judge ebooks in seconds. A book with clean formatting, consistent headings, and comfortable spacing feels trustworthy. A book with walls of text, random fonts, and broken page breaks feels amateur — even if the content is excellent.

Formatting is not about making your book pretty. It is about making it readable. And readability directly affects reviews, refunds, and word-of-mouth sales.

Step 1: Clean Up Your Manuscript

Before touching any formatting tool, clean the raw text:

  • Remove double spaces. Use find-and-replace to change double spaces to single spaces throughout.
  • Fix inconsistent line breaks. Extra blank lines between paragraphs should be removed — spacing is handled by styles, not empty lines.
  • Standardize quotes and dashes. Pick one style (curly quotes look more professional than straight quotes) and apply it everywhere.
  • Remove manual formatting. Clear all the random bold, italic, and font-size changes you made while drafting. You will reapply formatting properly with styles.

In Google Docs or Word, use "Clear formatting" on the entire document, then rebuild with styles. It feels like going backwards, but it saves hours later.

Step 2: Apply Proper Styles

This is the core of professional formatting. Instead of manually making text big and bold for headings, use heading styles.

The Essential Styles

Every ebook needs these:

  • Title style — for the book title page
  • Heading 1 — for chapter titles
  • Heading 2 — for sections within chapters
  • Heading 3 — for subsections (use sparingly)
  • Normal/Body — for all regular text
  • Quote style — for pull quotes or testimonials

Body Text Settings

For comfortable reading:

  • Font: Use a serif font (like Georgia or Times New Roman) for the body. Serif fonts are easier to read in long passages.
  • Size: 11pt or 12pt for PDF. For EPUB/Kindle, the reader controls the size, so just keep it standard.
  • Line spacing: 1.15 to 1.5. Cramped text is hard to read.
  • First-line indent: 0.3 inches for each new paragraph (standard for books), OR space between paragraphs — pick one, not both.
  • Alignment: Justified looks more professional for ebooks, though left-aligned is easier to read on screens. Either is fine if consistent.

Chapter Headings

Make chapter titles clearly distinct:

  • Larger font (18–24pt)
  • Bold
  • Start each chapter on a new page (use page breaks, not empty lines)
  • Consider a simple decorative element like a horizontal line under the title

Step 3: Add Front Matter and Back Matter

Professional ebooks have standard sections before and after the main content.

Front Matter (Before Chapter 1)

  1. Title page — book title, your name, nothing else
  2. Copyright page — "© 2026 [Your Name]. All rights reserved." plus a short disclaimer if your book gives business or financial advice
  3. Table of contents — generate this automatically from your heading styles (Google Docs and Word both do this)
  4. Introduction/Preface — optional, but recommended

    5. Back Matter (After the Last Chapter)

    1. Conclusion or final thoughts — if not already a chapter
    2. About the author — short bio with a photo if you like
    3. Call to action — your email list signup, YouTube channel, or next product
    4. Also by the author — list your other books (even if this is your first, leave the section ready)

      5. Step 4: Choose Your Formatting Tool

      Free Options

      Google Docs — Good enough for most first ebooks. Apply styles, insert a table of contents, export to PDF directly. For EPUB, you will need an extra step.

      Reedsy Book Editor (free) — A browser-based tool designed specifically for book formatting. Write or paste your manuscript, apply professional templates, and export to both PDF and EPUB with one click. This is the best free option for beginners.

      Kindle Create (free from Amazon) — Amazon's own tool. Import your Word document and it converts it into a Kindle-ready file with professional themes. If you plan to sell on Amazon KDP, this is the easiest path.

      Paid Options

      Vellum (Mac only, $199+) — The gold standard for indie authors. Beautiful templates, one-click export to every format. Worth it if you plan to publish multiple books.

      Atticus ($147 one-time) — Works on any computer, similar to Vellum. Good templates and multi-format export.

      Hiring a formatter ($50–$200 on Fiverr/Upwork) — If formatting feels overwhelming, outsourcing is affordable. Provide your clean manuscript and get back a professional file.

      My recommendation for beginners: Start with Reedsy (free) or Kindle Create (free). Upgrade only when you have proven sales.

      Step 5: Export to the Right Formats

      Different sales channels need different file formats:

      | Format | Used For | How to Create |

      |--------|----------|---------------|

      | PDF | Direct sales, Gumroad, your own website | Export from Google Docs, Word, or Reedsy |

      | EPUB | Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play Books | Export from Reedsy or Atticus/Vellum |

      | MOBI/KPF | Amazon Kindle (KDP) | Kindle Create or upload EPUB to KDP directly |

      | Print PDF | Paperback via KDP Print or local printers | Reedsy or Vellum with print settings |

      Important: Always test your exported file before selling. Open the PDF on your phone. Load the EPUB in a reader app. Check that the table of contents links work, images display correctly, and nothing looks broken.

      Common Formatting Mistakes to Avoid

      Using tabs or spaces for indentation. Always use paragraph styles for indents. Tabs break on different devices.

      Manually typing a table of contents. It will go out of sync the moment you edit anything. Always generate it from heading styles.

      Inconsistent heading levels. If Chapter 1 uses Heading 1, every chapter must use Heading 1. Check this before exporting.

      Tiny or huge fonts. Stick to standard sizes. Readers should not need to zoom to read your book.

      Forgetting mobile readers. Most ebook buyers in Bangladesh read on phones. Check your PDF on a phone screen — if it requires constant zooming, consider a reflowable EPUB instead.

      No page breaks between chapters. Chapters running together looks unprofessional. Insert a proper page break before each chapter title.

      A Simple Formatting Checklist

      Before you call your ebook done, verify:

      • [ ] All chapters use consistent Heading 1 style
      • [ ] Body text uses one font, one size throughout
      • [ ] Table of contents is auto-generated and links work
      • [ ] Each chapter starts on a new page
      • [ ] Front matter (title, copyright, TOC) is complete
      • [ ] Back matter (about author, CTA) is complete
      • [ ] File tested on phone and computer
      • [ ] No typos in chapter titles or headings
      • [ ] Images (if any) display correctly and are not pixelated

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Can I format my ebook in Google Docs alone?

      Yes, for PDF sales. Apply heading styles, generate a table of contents, and export to PDF. For EPUB or Kindle, use Reedsy or Kindle Create as an additional step — both are free.

      Should I hire someone to format my first ebook?

      Only if the technical side truly overwhelms you. Formatting is a one-time learnable skill, and the free tools are genuinely good now. Save the $50–$200 for your cover design or marketing instead.

      What is the difference between EPUB and PDF?

      PDF has a fixed layout — it looks the same on every device but requires zooming on small screens. EPUB is reflowable — text adjusts to the reader's screen and font preferences. For phone readers, EPUB is better. For workbooks with precise layouts, PDF is better.

      How do I add images to my ebook?

      Insert images at a reasonable resolution (150–300 DPI for print, 72 DPI is fine for screen-only ebooks). Keep file sizes manageable — huge images make downloads slow. Always check that images appear correctly in the exported file.

      Do I need different formatting for Amazon vs direct sales?

      The interior formatting is mostly the same. Amazon KDP accepts EPUB or Word files and converts them. For direct sales (your website, Gumroad), PDF is most common.
