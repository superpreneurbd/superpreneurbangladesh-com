Every week, someone in Bangladesh sees a YouTube ad promising easy money: "Launch a Shopify store, sell to America, and watch the dollars roll in." The guru is standing in front of a rented car. The screenshots show six-figure months. Here is what they don't tell you: from Bangladesh, that model is one of the hardest online businesses you can attempt. This guide tells you the truth — and shows you the versions of dropshipping that actually work here.

Why the Shopify-to-USA Fantasy Rarely Works

Dropshipping means you sell a product you don't stock. The customer orders from you, you order from a supplier, and the supplier ships directly to the customer. You keep the margin between the supplier's price and your selling price. The model is real. The problem is the specific version that gurus sell to Bangladeshis.

The payment gateway wall

To sell internationally, you need to accept card payments. Shopify Payments is not available in Bangladesh. Stripe is not available either. International payment processors like 2Checkout or Payoneer checkout options can work in some setups, but they are harder to set up, often require a registered business, and come with higher fees and frozen-fund risks. Most Bangladeshi-run "Shopify dropshipping" attempts stall right here — the store is ready, but taking money from customers is a struggle.

The ad cost math

Dropshippers selling to the USA run Facebook or TikTok ads. Customer acquisition costs in the US are high — many beginners spend hundreds of dollars before their first sale. A Bangladeshi beginner with a $50 budget gets almost no data and no sales, then concludes "dropshipping is a scam." It isn't a scam. The math just doesn't work with a small test budget in an expensive market.

The trust gap

US customers buy from stores they trust. A new store with an unfamiliar brand, long shipping times from a Chinese supplier, and no reviews loses to established sellers. Building that trust takes time and money most beginners don't have.

What Dropshipping Looks Like in Bangladesh (The Realistic Version)

Forget Shopify-to-USA for now. The models that work from Bangladesh sell to Bangladeshi customers, in taka, with cash on delivery (COD).

Model 1: Local-supplier dropshipping with COD via Facebook

This is the most practical entry point. You find a local wholesaler or importer, list their products on a Facebook page or a simple website, and take orders with cash on delivery. When an order comes in, the supplier ships it — you pay the supplier only after collecting payment from the customer.

How it works step by step:

  1. Find suppliers in wholesale markets (Dhaka's Chawkbazar, New Market importers, or wholesale groups on Facebook). Ask for their reseller terms — many already work with online resellers.
  2. Create a Facebook page around one niche: phone accessories, kitchen gadgets, or women's fashion, for example.
  3. Post product photos and short videos. Facebook video and reels get far more reach than static posts.
  4. Run small Facebook ads (500–1,000 BDT per day to start) or build organic reach through consistent posting and groups.
  5. Take orders through Facebook Messenger or a simple order form. Collect the customer's name, phone number, and address.
  6. Confirm every order by phone before shipping — this cuts return rates sharply.
  7. Send the order through a courier like RedX, Pathao, or Steadfast, using COD.
  8. Pay the supplier after delivery and keep your margin.

    9. Model 2: Buy wholesale, resell at retail

    This is technically "reselling," not pure dropshipping, but it's how most successful Bangladeshi online sellers actually operate. You buy a small batch of products from a wholesaler (even 20–50 units), store them at home, and sell them through Facebook or a simple site. You control packaging, delivery speed, and quality — which means fewer returns and happier customers. Start with one product category and a budget you can afford to lose.

    Model 3: Pre-order model for imported items

    If you want to sell imported products (electronics, gadgets), use a pre-order model. Customers pay a deposit, you consolidate orders, import through a courier or buying agent, and deliver. This removes inventory risk. It requires trust, so it works best after you already have some reputation — don't start here as a total beginner.

    Realistic Margins (No Hype)

    • Local dropshipping margins typically run 100–300 BDT per order on small items, more on higher-ticket products. Volume matters more than per-order profit.
    • Return rates are real. COD return rates in Bangladesh often run 10–25% or higher. Customers order and don't pick up. Every returned parcel costs you courier fees with no revenue. Budget for this.
    • Ad costs rise over time. A campaign that works today can die in weeks as competition enters. You must keep testing new products and new creatives.
    • This is not passive income. Running a product business means handling supplier issues, angry customers, courier delays, and returned parcels. It is a real business with real headaches.

    Common Scams and Pitfalls

    • Paid "guru" courses. Anyone selling you a 5,000–20,000 BDT course promising "secrets" of dropshipping is making money from the course, not from dropshipping. The honest version of everything in this article is free. Paid courses about this topic rarely contain anything you can't learn from free YouTube videos and actual practice.
    • Supplier fraud. Never pay large advances to suppliers you haven't met or verified. Start with tiny orders, verify quality, then scale.
    • Screenshot hype. Revenue screenshots prove nothing. Profit screenshots, after ad costs, returns, and courier fees, are the only numbers that matter — and gurus never show those.
    • Copying trending products blindly. By the time a product is "trending" on every guru's channel, the market is saturated. Find your own angles: local problems, underserved niches, better service.

    A Small-Budget Start Plan (30 Days)

    Week 1: Pick one niche and find suppliers. Choose something you understand — phone accessories, home organizers, baby products. Visit or message 5–10 suppliers. Compare prices and terms.

    Week 2: Set up your sales channel. Create a Facebook page with a clean profile, professional cover, and product catalog. Set up an order form (Google Form works fine at the start) and choose a courier.

    Week 3: Test with a tiny budget. Start with 2–3 products and 500 BDT/day in Facebook ads. Your goal is data, not profit — learn which products get clicks and messages. Confirm orders by phone.

    Week 4: Evaluate and decide. Did you get orders? What was your real profit after ads, courier, and returns? If the numbers are positive, double down on the winning product. If not, switch products or niches — don't switch models yet.

    Start with 10,000–20,000 BDT total. That is enough to learn the game without hurting yourself if it fails. Never borrow money to start dropshipping.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can I do dropshipping in Bangladesh without a website?

    Yes. Most Bangladeshi sellers start with just a Facebook page and Messenger. A website adds credibility later, but it's not required to make your first sales.

    Is Shopify available in Bangladesh?

    You can create a Shopify store from Bangladesh, but Shopify Payments is not available here, which makes accepting international card payments difficult. For selling to local customers, a Facebook page or a simple local site is more practical.

    How much money do I need to start?

    A realistic starting budget is 10,000–20,000 BDT for local dropshipping — covering small ad tests, courier deposits, and phone costs. Pure dropshipping needs almost no inventory money, but ads and returns cost real cash.

    Is dropshipping legal in Bangladesh?

    Selling products online is legal. Registering a trade license becomes necessary as you grow, especially for bank accounts and formal courier contracts. Start small, formalize as revenue grows.

    Why do so many dropshipping courses target Bangladeshis?

    Because selling the dream is profitable. Courses sell hope to people who want fast money. The course creators profit from the courses, not necessarily from dropshipping itself. Learn from free resources and real practice instead.

    What is the biggest reason beginners fail?

    Two reasons: unrealistic expectations (expecting instant profit) and ignoring the return-rate math on COD orders.
