F-commerce — selling through Facebook — is one of the most proven online business models in Bangladesh. Thousands of small businesses run entirely on Facebook pages. No website needed. No app needed. Just a page, products people want, and reliable delivery.

This guide gives you the full step-by-step: setting up your page properly, sourcing products from wholesale markets, pricing for cash-on-delivery, handling payments and couriers, serving customers in Bangla, and scaling with Facebook ads. Everything here reflects how f-commerce actually works in Bangladesh in 2026.

Step 1: Set Up a Proper Facebook Page

Do not sell from your personal profile. A business page looks professional, gives you access to ads and insights, and keeps your personal life separate.

Setup checklist:

  • Create a Facebook Page (not a group, not a profile) with a clear business name. Avoid names that are hard to spell or remember.
  • Add a clean logo or product photo as the profile picture and a banner showing your best products.
  • Fill in the About section completely: what you sell, delivery areas, contact number, and WhatsApp link.
  • Turn on the Shop section if it is available in your region, or use pinned posts and photo albums as your catalog.
  • Set up auto-replies in Messenger for common questions (price, delivery charge, delivery time). Many customers message at midnight — auto-replies keep them engaged until you respond.
  • Add a WhatsApp button. A large share of Bangladeshi customers prefer to order over WhatsApp after finding you on Facebook.

Trust signals matter. New pages with blurry photos and no reviews get ignored. Ask early customers to leave reviews. Post real photos of your products, not just supplier images. Trust is the currency of f-commerce.

Step 2: Choose What to Sell

Pick products with three qualities: proven demand, reasonable margins, and manageable logistics.

Categories that consistently work in Bangladesh: women's fashion (three-pieces, hijabs, kurtis, jewelry), cosmetics and skincare, baby products and toys, home and kitchen gadgets, men's fashion (panjabis, t-shirts, watches), and Islamic products (prayer mats, attar, tasbih).

How to validate before you invest: Search Facebook for pages selling the same product. If dozens of pages sell it with active comments and recent posts, demand exists. If nobody sells it, that is either an opportunity or a warning — check whether people actually search for it.

Start with ONE category, ideally 5–10 products. Beginners who list 50 random products confuse customers and themselves. Depth beats breadth: become the page people trust for one thing.

Step 3: Source Products from Wholesale Markets

You do not need to import from China on day one. Dhaka's wholesale markets give you low minimum orders and same-day stock.

Key markets:

  • Islampur — the biggest wholesale hub for clothing, fabrics, and fashion accessories. Best prices, but you need to buy in reasonable quantities.
  • New Market / Gausia area — wholesale and semi-wholesale for clothing, cosmetics, and accessories. Good for smaller test orders.
  • Chawkbazar — wholesale for cosmetics, electronics accessories, toys, and household items.
  • Bangabazar — clothing at very low prices; quality varies, so inspect carefully.

Sourcing tips:

  • Visit in person the first few times. Photos from suppliers lie; your eyes do not.
  • Buy small test quantities first (10–20 pieces per item). Only reorder what sells.
  • Build relationships with 2–3 reliable suppliers. A supplier who answers your calls and holds stock for you is worth more than a slightly cheaper price.
  • Check return/replacement policies for defective items before you pay.

As you grow, you can move to importing for better margins — our guide on importing from China to Bangladesh covers that step.

Step 4: Price for Profit (With COD in Mind)

Most Bangladeshi online orders are Cash on Delivery (COD). That shapes your pricing.

A simple pricing formula:

  1. Product cost (what you paid the supplier)
  2. + Packaging cost
  3. + Average delivery cost (Dhaka vs. outside Dhaka differs — use a blended average)
  4. + Expected return/cancellation loss (assume 10–20% of COD orders fail — customer unreachable, refused, wrong address)
  5. + Your marketing cost per order
  6. + Your profit margin (aim for at least 30–40% on the product cost)

    7. Example: A kurti costs you BDT 400 wholesale. Packaging BDT 20. Delivery averages BDT 100. Marketing costs BDT 80 per order. Base cost: BDT 600. Adjust for 15% order failure: 600 ÷ 0.85 ≈ BDT 705. Add 35% margin: ≈ BDT 950. So price around BDT 950–1,000.

    If the market will not pay that, find a cheaper supplier or pick a different product. Selling at a loss to "get customers" is how f-commerce businesses die quietly.

    COD failure is your biggest hidden cost. Confirm every order by phone or WhatsApp before dispatching. A 2-minute confirmation call cuts failed deliveries dramatically.

    Step 5: Handle Payments (bKash, Nagad, COD)

    Offer multiple payment options:

    • Cash on Delivery — still the default for most customers. Expect it on 70–80% of orders.
    • bKash / Nagad advance — offer a small discount (BDT 30–50 off) for advance payment. This improves your cash flow and eliminates failed-delivery losses on those orders.
    • bKash / Nagad / Rocket — have all major mobile banking numbers ready and clearly listed.

    Keep clean records. A simple spreadsheet with order date, customer name, phone, product, amount, payment status, and courier tracking number is enough to start. Messy records are how money silently leaks.

    Step 6: Deliver with Courier Services

    You do not need your own delivery fleet. Bangladesh has several courier services built for e-commerce:

    • Pathao Courier — wide coverage, app-based booking, COD collection service
    • RedX — popular with f-commerce sellers, strong outside-Dhaka coverage
    • eCourier — one of the earliest e-commerce couriers, reliable tracking
    • Sundarban Courier — extensive nationwide network, good for remote areas

    How to choose: Test 2–3 services with real orders. Compare delivery speed, COD remittance time (how fast they return your cash), customer support responsiveness, and coverage in your main customer areas. The cheapest courier is not the best if they lose parcels or delay your COD money by weeks.

    Pack products well. A damaged product that looked premium in photos destroys trust. Use poly mailers for clothing and bubble wrap + boxes for fragile items. Your packaging is part of your brand.

    Step 7: Customer Service in Bangla

    Most of your customers will communicate in Bangla (often Banglish). Serve them in the language they use.

    Customer service rules that win repeat buyers:

    • Reply fast. Under 15 minutes during waking hours is the goal. Slow replies lose sales to competitors.
    • Be polite and patient, even with difficult customers. One angry customer posts screenshots; ten happy ones tell nobody.
    • Confirm orders before shipping, and follow up after delivery ("Apnar product peyechen?"). A simple "Kemon laglo?" message turns one-time buyers into repeat customers.
    • Handle complaints quickly. A replacement costs less than a bad review that scares off fifty future customers.

    Save common answers as quick replies in Messenger or WhatsApp Business: price lists, delivery charges by area, delivery time estimates, return policy. This saves hours daily.

    Step 8: Handle Returns Gracefully

    You need a clear, written return policy posted on your page. Something like: "7-day easy replacement for defective or wrong items. Customer must send unboxing video for damage claims."

    Why unboxing videos: They protect you from false damage claims and protect honest customers when products genuinely arrive broken. Make it a standard request, not an accusation.

    Accept that returns cost money. Price them into your margins (Step 4) instead of fighting every return. Sellers with fair return policies get more orders overall — the math works in your favor.

    Step 9: Scale with Facebook Ads (Basics)

    Once you have 20–30 organic sales and know which products convert, ads can multiply your reach.

    Beginner ads framework:

    1. Start with BDT 300–500 per day on ONE winning product.
    2. Use a simple video or carousel ad showing the product in real use — not just studio photos.
    3. Target broadly at first (women 22–45 in Dhaka + Chattogram for fashion, for example), then narrow based on what the data shows.
    4. Send ad traffic to Messenger or WhatsApp, not just the page. Conversations convert better than passive browsing.
    5. Track one number: cost per order. If your cost per order is below your profit per order, increase the budget slowly. If it is above, fix the product, price, or creative before spending more.

      6. Warning: Do not run ads before your fulfillment is solid. Ads multiply everything — including bad reviews from late deliveries. Get your operations right on organic orders first.

      Realistic Startup Budget

      Here is what starting f-commerce honestly costs in BDT:

      | Item | Budget range |

      |---|---|

      | Initial stock (test quantities) | 15,000–40,000 |

      | Packaging materials | 2,000–5,000 |

      | Facebook page setup (design, photos) | 0–5,000 (DIY vs. hired) |

      | Initial ad testing budget | 10,000–20,000 |

      | Working cash (COD float, emergencies) | 10,000–20,000 |

      | Total realistic range | 37,000–90,000 |

      You can start leaner (BDT 20,000–30,000) with a tiny product range and purely organic posting, but growth will be slow. Whatever you do, do not spend your entire budget on stock with nothing left for ads and operations.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      How much money do I need to start f-commerce in Bangladesh?

      A realistic starting budget is BDT 37,000–90,000, covering initial stock, packaging, ad testing, and working cash. You can start leaner at BDT 20,000–30,000 with a small product range and organic-only marketing, but expect slower growth.

      Do I need a trade license to sell on Facebook in Bangladesh?

      For a small home-based start, most sellers begin without one. But as you grow — especially if you want to open a bank account in the business name, run larger ad spends, or formalize — getting a trade license from your local city corporation or union parishad is inexpensive and worth doing. It also builds trust with suppliers.

      Which courier is best for f-commerce in Bangladesh?

      Pathao, RedX, and eCourier are the most popular among f-commerce sellers. The "best" depends on your delivery areas and how fast they remit COD payments. Test 2–3 services with real orders before committing your volume to one.

      How do I handle customers who refuse COD orders?

      Confirm every order by phone or WhatsApp before dispatching — this single habit cuts refusal rates dramatically. Price a 10–20% failure rate into your margins from the start so refusals hurt less. For repeat offenders (same number ordering and refusing), keep a blacklist.

      Can I do f-commerce without showing my face?

      Yes. Many successful pages never show the owner's face — they show products, packaging, customer reviews, and behind-the-scenes clips of packing orders. But pages with a human presence generally build trust faster. Even hands-only packing videos help.

      When should I move from f-commerce to my own website?

      Consider a website when you have consistent daily orders (20+ per day), repeat customers asking for easier ordering, and ad spend above BDT 30,000–50,000/month. A website adds credibility and lets you run retargeting properly — but it also adds costs, so only move when the volume justifies it. ## The Bottom Line F-commerce works because it meets Bangladeshi customers where they already are — on Facebook, paying cash on delivery, chatting in Bangla. Start with one product category, source smart from wholesale markets, price honestly with COD failures in mind, deliver reliably, and serve customers well. Scale with ads only after the fundamentals are solid.
