Your ebook is selling. Readers love it. Now what?

The smartest next move is not another ebook - it is a course built on the same foundation. Your ebook proved the topic has demand. Your readers proved they will pay to learn from you. A course lets you serve the same audience at a higher price point with deeper transformation.

This guide shows you exactly how to expand from ebook to course without starting from scratch.

Why a Course Is the Natural Next Step

Higher Prices, Same Audience

Ebooks typically sell for ৳299-৳999. Online courses sell for ৳1,999-৳9,999 or more. Same topic, same audience, 5-10x the revenue per customer.

The price difference is justified: courses offer video instruction, structured learning paths, community access, and often direct support. Buyers pay more because they get more.

Deeper Transformation Commands Premium Pricing

An ebook teaches. A course transforms. The difference is guidance, accountability, and interaction:

  • Ebook: Reader learns at their own pace, alone
  • Course: Student follows a structured path, watches demonstrations, completes exercises, gets feedback

For topics where doing matters more than knowing (freelancing, design, marketing), courses deliver results that ebooks cannot match alone.

You Already Did the Hard Work

Your ebook contains the curriculum. The research is done. The structure exists. The examples are written. Turning it into a course is repurposing, not starting over.

Most authors find that 70-80% of their course content already exists in their ebook. The remaining work is adding the elements that make courses different: video, exercises, and community.

When to Expand (And When to Wait)

Expand When:

  • Your ebook has consistent sales (even modest ones prove demand)
  • Readers ask for more depth, examples, or personal help
  • You see the same questions repeatedly (these become course modules)
  • You have 5-10 hours per week for course creation
  • Your ebook has 20+ positive reviews (social proof transfers)

Wait When:

  • Your ebook is not selling yet (fix the ebook first)
  • You cannot commit to supporting students (courses require more involvement)
  • The topic does not benefit from video or interaction (some topics are better as books)
  • You are burned out from the ebook launch (rest first, then expand)

The Repurposing Framework: Ebook to Course

Step 1: Map Chapters to Modules

Each ebook chapter becomes a course module. A 10-chapter ebook becomes a 10-module course. This gives you the skeleton instantly.

Example mapping:

| Ebook Chapter | Course Module | Added Elements |

|---------------|---------------|----------------|

| Choosing your skill | Module 1: Skill selection | Video walkthrough, self-assessment quiz |

| Setting up profiles | Module 2: Profile setup | Screen recordings, profile templates |

| Writing proposals | Module 3: Proposals that win | Proposal teardowns, practice exercises |

| Getting first clients | Module 4: Client acquisition | Role-play videos, outreach scripts |

Step 2: Add What Books Cannot Do

For each module, add course-specific elements:

Video instruction. Record yourself explaining each concept. You do not need fancy equipment - a phone camera, decent lighting (window light works), and clear audio (a ৳1,500 lavalier microphone) are enough to start.

Screen recordings. For process-based topics, record your screen as you demonstrate. Free tools: OBS Studio, Loom (free tier). Show, do not just tell.

Exercises and worksheets. Each module should have one action step. "After watching this module, do X." Worksheets turn passive watching into active learning.

Quizzes. Short 3-5 question quizzes after each module reinforce learning and give students a sense of progress.

Step 3: Add Community and Support

This is the biggest differentiator from an ebook:

  • Private Facebook/Discord group for students (free to set up)
  • Weekly Q&A sessions (even 30 minutes on Facebook Live)
  • Email support for stuck students (set boundaries: e.g., 48-hour response time)

Community is often cited as the most valuable part of a course. Students learn from each other, stay motivated, and get unstuck faster.

Choosing a Course Platform

International Platforms

Udemy: Massive marketplace, built-in traffic. But: heavy discounting culture ($10-15 typical sale price), you do not own the customer relationship, and revenue share is low for marketplace sales.

Teachable: You control pricing and own student data. Monthly fee ($39+/month) or transaction fees on free plan. Good for independent creators.

Gumroad: Can host video courses alongside ebooks. Simple, no monthly fee. Less course-specific features than Teachable.

For Bangladeshi Audiences

Your own website (WordPress + LMS plugin): LearnDash or Tutor LMS (free version available) turn WordPress into a course platform. Combine with bKash/Nagad payments via SSLCommerz. Full control, local payments, no monthly platform fee.

Facebook groups + Google Drive: The scrappy Bangladeshi standard. Course videos in a private Facebook group or shared Drive folder, payments via bKash. Zero platform cost, but manual management and less professional.

Local course platforms: Several Bangladeshi edtech platforms host creator courses. Research their commission rates and audience fit before committing.

Platform Decision Framework

| Your Situation | Best Choice |

|----------------|-------------|

| Testing the concept, minimal investment | Facebook group + bKash |

| Serious about courses, want professionalism | WordPress + Tutor LMS + SSLCommerz |

| Targeting international students | Teachable or Gumroad |

| Want marketplace traffic | Udemy (accept lower margins) |

Pricing Your Course

Course pricing follows the same psychology as ebook pricing, at higher numbers:

Bangladesh market ranges:

  • Mini-course (2-3 hours): ৳999-৳1,999
  • Full course (8-15 hours): ৳2,999-৳7,999
  • Flagship program (with support/community): ৳7,999-৳19,999

Pricing rule of thumb: Your course should cost 5-10x your ebook. If your ebook is ৳499, your course should be ৳2,499-৳4,999. This makes the ebook feel like a no-brainer entry point and the course feel like the premium transformation.

Bundle strategy: Offer ebook + course together at a discount. "Get the ebook (৳499) + full video course (৳3,999) for ৳3,499." Bundles increase average order value significantly.

The Product Ladder: Your Long-Term Strategy

Think beyond a single course. Build a ladder where each product leads to the next:

  1. Free content (YouTube, blog) - builds audience
  2. Lead magnet (free chapter) - captures emails
  3. Ebook (৳499) - first purchase, low risk
  4. Mini-course (৳1,999) - deeper dive on one topic
  5. Flagship course (৳5,999) - complete transformation
  6. Coaching/consulting (৳15,000+) - personal guidance

    7. Each rung qualifies buyers for the next. Someone who bought your ebook is 10x more likely to buy your course than a cold visitor. Someone who completed your course is your best coaching prospect.

    You do not need to build the entire ladder at once. Start with what you have (probably steps 1-3), then add one rung at a time.

    Launching Your Course to Ebook Buyers

    Your existing ebook buyers are your warmest launch audience:

    1. Announce early. Tell ebook buyers the course is coming. Build anticipation.
    2. Offer founder pricing. "As an ebook reader, get 40% off the course launch price." Rewards loyalty and drives fast initial sales.
    3. Ask for beta testers. Offer free access to 10-20 ebook buyers in exchange for feedback and testimonials. Their input improves the course before public launch.
    4. Use their language. The questions ebook buyers asked you become your course marketing copy. You already know their objections.

      5. Frequently Asked Questions

      How long should my course be?

      Long enough to deliver the transformation, short enough to complete. For most topics, 5-10 hours of video content is the sweet spot. Longer courses have lower completion rates. Focus on outcomes, not hours.

      Do I need to be on camera?

      No, but it helps build connection. Screen recordings with voiceover work well for technical topics. If you are uncomfortable on camera, start with voiceover and add talking-head segments later.

      What if my ebook did not sell well?

      Do not build a course on an unproven topic. Fix the ebook's positioning, marketing, or topic first. A course amplifies whatever the ebook does - including poor product-market fit.

      Can I sell the course before creating it?

      Yes - this is called pre-selling. Offer the course at a discount, collect payments, then create it over 4-6 weeks with student input. This validates demand before you invest creation time. Be transparent about the timeline.

      Should the course replace the ebook?

      No. They serve different buyers at different price points. Keep both.
