Every restaurant, coaching center, clinic, and small business in Bangladesh eventually needs a website. And most of those websites run on WordPress. That makes WordPress freelancing one of the steadiest ways to earn online from Bangladesh — bigger projects, higher prices, and clients who keep paying you month after month. Imtiaz Khan recommends it because the learning curve is honest: you can earn with no code at all, then grow into a real developer over time.

Why WordPress Freelancing Still Pays

Skeptics say website builders killed web freelancing. They are wrong, and here is why:

  • Most small business sites run on WordPress. It powers a huge share of the web, and Bangladeshi businesses trust it because every freelancer knows it.
  • Business owners do not want to build it themselves. They want to run their restaurant, not learn page builders. They will pay someone to handle it.
  • Websites need ongoing care. Updates, backups, security, small changes — this creates recurring monthly income, which most freelance skills do not offer.
  • Projects are bigger. One website project can pay what 20 Facebook post designs pay. Fewer clients, more income per client.

What You Actually Need to Learn (vs. What You Can Skip)

Beginners drown in tutorials. Here is the honest split:

Learn first (the earning core):

  • How WordPress works: posts vs. pages, menus, media library, user roles
  • Installing and configuring themes
  • Elementor or another page builder (this is your main building tool)
  • Essential plugins: contact forms, SEO (Rank Math), caching, backups, security
  • Basic hosting and DNS: pointing a domain to hosting, installing WordPress, SSL certificates
  • Security basics: updates, strong passwords, backup routines

Learn later (the developer path):

  • PHP basics and WordPress theme structure
  • Custom theme development
  • Custom plugins
  • Advanced performance optimization
  • JavaScript for interactive features

Skip at first:

  • Building your own CMS or frameworks from scratch
  • Advanced server administration
  • Anything that does not help you deliver a client site this month

The rule: learn what the next paying project requires. Nothing more.

The No-Code Path: Build Sites with Elementor, Charge for Setup

Here is the fastest route to income. You do not need to write a single line of code:

  1. Learn Elementor deeply. It is a drag-and-drop page builder for WordPress. Two to three weeks of daily practice makes you competent.
  2. Learn the surrounding skills. Domain + hosting setup, SSL, contact forms, WhatsApp chat buttons, Google Maps embeds, basic SEO setup. Clients expect a complete site, not just pretty pages.
  3. Build 3 demo sites. A restaurant site with menu and reservation form. A coaching center site with course listings. A portfolio site for a photographer. These are your portfolio.
  4. Sell website setups. You are selling a finished business asset: "I will build your complete business website — 5 pages, contact form, WhatsApp button, Google Maps, basic SEO — ready in 7 days."

    5. This is real freelancing. Thousands of Bangladeshi freelancers earn this way. The client does not care whether you wrote code; they care that their business is online and customers can reach them.

    The Developer Path: PHP and Custom Themes Later

    The no-code path has a ceiling: complex custom features, unique designs, and high-budget clients need real development. When you are ready:

    1. Learn PHP basics. WordPress is built on PHP. You do not need to master it — learn enough to read theme files and make small customizations.
    2. Learn child themes. Customize existing themes safely without losing changes on update.
    3. Build one custom theme. Even a simple one teaches you the full WordPress template structure.
    4. Learn custom post types and fields. This is what separates basic builders from developers — custom solutions for specific business needs.

      5. Do this while no-code projects pay your bills. Never pause earning to "become a real developer first." The market pays for delivered sites, not for credentials.

      Portfolio Approach: Build 3 Demo Sites

      Clients buy websites by looking at websites. Your portfolio needs live demo sites, not screenshots:

      • Demo 1: A local business site (restaurant, salon, or clinic). Show services, gallery, contact, map.
      • Demo 2: A service business site (coaching center, consultancy, or agency). Show courses/services, testimonials section, lead capture form.
      • Demo 3: A simple online store or a blog-style site. Show you can handle products or content.

      Host them on a cheap shared hosting account or even free staging. Put all three links on one simple portfolio page with a short description of what each site includes. Three live sites beat thirty screenshots.

      For general portfolio strategy, see building a freelance portfolio from zero.

      Finding Clients

      Local businesses first. This is the best channel for WordPress beginners. Every town in Bangladesh has businesses without websites or with terrible ones. Approach them with a specific observation: "I noticed your restaurant has no website — customers searching on Google find your competitors instead." Show one demo site on your phone. Local trust closes deals faster than any platform.

      Upwork. Search for "WordPress website," "Elementor," "WordPress bug fix." Bug fixes and small customizations are great entry jobs — they are small, well-defined, and build your reviews fast. Then move up to full site builds.

      Referrals. Website clients know other business owners. Every finished project should end with: "Do you know any business owner who needs a website?" Offer a small referral thank-you.

      Facebook groups. Bangladeshi business groups regularly have website requests. Answer with your demo links, not just "I am interested."

      Strong proposals matter here more than anywhere — a website is a big purchase for a small business. Read how to write proposals that win clients.

      Pricing Per Site in BDT and USD

      Realistic ranges for Bangladesh-based freelancers:

      • Basic business website (5–7 pages, Elementor): 15,000–40,000 BDT locally; $200–$600 on Upwork starting out.
      • Standard business site (10–15 pages, blog, advanced forms): 40,000–80,000 BDT locally; $600–$1,500 internationally with experience.
      • E-commerce site (WooCommerce): 50,000–150,000 BDT locally depending on products and features; $1,000–$3,000+ internationally.
      • Bug fixes and small tasks: 1,000–5,000 BDT per fix locally; $20–$100 per task on Upwork.

      Price the project, not your hours. A restaurant website that brings customers is worth far more than the hours you spent building it. As your portfolio grows, raise prices with every 3–4 projects.

      Maintenance Retainers: Recurring Monthly Income

      This is WordPress freelancing's secret weapon. Websites need ongoing care:

      • WordPress, theme, and plugin updates
      • Daily or weekly backups
      • Security monitoring
      • Small content changes (new photos, price updates, new pages)
      • Uptime monitoring

      Offer a monthly care plan: 2,000–8,000 BDT per month per site locally (more for e-commerce). Ten sites on care plans = 20,000–80,000 BDT of predictable monthly income before you take any new project. Sell the care plan at handover: "Your site is live. For ৳3,000/month I keep it updated, backed up, and secure — you never touch it."

      This recurring base is what turns freelancing from feast-or-famine into a real business.

      Honest Learning Timeline

      No hype. Here is what the path actually looks like with 1–2 hours of daily practice:

      • Weeks 1–2: WordPress basics — install it, learn the dashboard, publish pages and posts.
      • Weeks 3–5: Elementor — build practice pages daily until it feels natural.
      • Weeks 6–8: Plugins, hosting, DNS, SSL, forms, backups — the full setup workflow.
      • Weeks 9–12: Build your 3 demo sites. Start pitching local businesses in week 10.
      • Months 4–6: First 2–5 paying projects. Expect them to be messy — that is normal.
      • Months 6–12: Raise prices, add care plans, start learning PHP alongside client work.

      One year from starting, a consistent learner can have a real client base and recurring income. It is not overnight, but it is one of the most durable freelance skills you can build.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Do I need to know coding to start WordPress freelancing?

      No. The Elementor no-code path lets you deliver complete client websites without writing code. Learn coding later to raise your ceiling, not to start earning.

      Is WordPress dying? Should I learn something newer instead?

      WordPress powers a massive share of business websites and is not going anywhere for small-business sites. Newer tools exist, but client demand for WordPress work in Bangladesh remains strong and steady.

      How much does it cost to start?

      Very little. A domain costs around 1,000–1,500 BDT per year, shared hosting 3,000–6,000 BDT per year. WordPress, Elementor (free version), and essential plugins are free. You can practice everything locally on your computer for free before spending anything.

      Can I do WordPress work on a low-end laptop?

      Yes. WordPress runs in a browser; Elementor works on any modern laptop. You do not need a powerful machine until much later, if ever.

      How do I compete with cheap $50 website sellers on Fiverr?

      Do not. Compete locally and on Upwork where clients value communication and reliability. Local Bangladeshi businesses pay more than Fiverr's race-to-the-bottom, and they become long-term care-plan clients.

      What is the single most profitable WordPress service?

      Monthly care plans. A site build pays once; a care plan pays every month.
