Recruiters hiring remotely search LinkedIn with keywords like "SEO specialist Bangladesh" or "virtual assistant Dhaka." If your profile doesn't contain those keywords — or if it looks empty and inactive — you're invisible. An optimized LinkedIn profile works while you sleep: recruiters find you, not the other way around.

This is the exact optimization checklist, section by section.

Profile Photo

Use a real photo. Not a logo, not a landscape, not a wedding picture cropped awkwardly.

  • Clear headshot, facing the camera, plain background
  • Professional dress (whatever "professional" means in your field)
  • Good lighting — stand near a window, it's free
  • Smile slightly. You want to look approachable, not intimidating.

Profiles with photos get dramatically more views than those without. This is the easiest win on the list.

Headline (The Most Important Line)

Your headline is what appears in search results. Most Bangladeshis waste it on their job title alone.

Weak: "Digital Marketing Executive at XYZ Company"

Strong: "Facebook & Google Ads Specialist | Helping E-commerce Brands Scale | Dhaka, Bangladesh | Open to Remote"

The formula: what you do + who you help + location + availability signal.

Keyword rule: Include the exact terms recruiters search for. If you want SEO jobs, your headline must contain "SEO." If you want VA work, it must contain "Virtual Assistant." LinkedIn search is literal — it matches words, not intentions.

About Section (Your Pitch)

Write in first person. 3-5 short paragraphs:

  1. What you do (one line, plain language)
  2. Proof (2-3 specific results with numbers)
  3. Who you help (your ideal client or employer type)
  4. Call to action (how to contact you)

    5. Example:

    "I'm a Facebook ads specialist based in Dhaka, working with e-commerce brands in the UK and Australia.

    In the last 2 years, I've managed ৳50L+ in ad spend at an average 3.2x ROAS. I specialize in creative testing and audience scaling for fashion and beauty brands.

    I help DTC founders who are spending on ads but not sure what's working. If that's you, my inbox is open.

    📩 Email: your@email.com | 🕐 Available 2pm–10pm BDT"

    Don't write: A third-person corporate bio ("Mr. X is a results-driven professional..."). Nobody talks like that, and recruiters skip it.

    Experience Section

    Same rules as your CV: action verb + what you did + measurable result. But LinkedIn gives you more space — use it.

    For each role, add:

    • 3-5 achievement bullets with metrics
    • Media: links to portfolio pieces, case studies, or relevant work
    • Skills tags (these feed LinkedIn's skill search)

    Freelance work counts. Add it as: "Freelance Digital Marketer | Self-employed." List client results (anonymized if needed). Freelance history is proof of self-management — exactly what remote employers want to see.

    Skills Section (SEO for Your Profile)

    Add 15-20 relevant skills. LinkedIn lets connections endorse them, but more importantly, recruiters filter by skills.

    Prioritize: The exact skills from job posts you want. If remote VA jobs list "calendar management," "inbox management," and "Notion," those exact phrases should be in your skills.

    Pin your top 3. LinkedIn lets you feature 3 skills at the top — choose the ones most relevant to your target roles.

    Recommendations

    Ask 3-5 people for LinkedIn recommendations: former managers, clients, colleagues. A short, specific recommendation ("Imtiaz managed our ad account for 8 months and cut our cost-per-lead by 40%") is worth more than a paragraph of generic praise.

    How to ask: Send a polite message with a suggested draft. Most people are happy to recommend you but don't know what to write — making it easy doubles your success rate.

    Activity: Be Visible

    An empty profile looks abandoned. Post or engage weekly:

    • Comment thoughtfully on posts in your industry (this gets you noticed by the poster's network)
    • Share one insight per week about your work (a lesson learned, a result, a tool you like)
    • Repost interesting industry news with your take

    You don't need to be an influencer. Consistent, genuine activity signals "this person is engaged and professional" — which is all recruiters need to see.

    Open to Work Settings

    Turn on "Open to Work" — but use it strategically:

    • For recruiters only (not public) if you're currently employed and don't want your boss to know
    • Public if you're actively job hunting and want maximum visibility
    • Specify: job titles you want, locations (including "Remote"), and job types

    Networking: Connect with Purpose

    Don't just collect connections. Each week:

    1. Connect with 10-15 people in your target industry (hiring managers, agency founders, fellow professionals)
    2. Always add a note: "Hi [Name], I'm a [your role] based in Dhaka. I followed your work on [specific thing]. Would love to connect."
    3. Engage with their posts before asking for anything

      4. The 80/20 of LinkedIn networking: Give value (comments, shares, introductions) 80% of the time. Ask for help 20% of the time. People help givers.

      Keywords Recruiters Actually Search

      Based on common remote hiring patterns, make sure your profile contains:

      • Your core skill terms (SEO, Facebook Ads, Virtual Assistant, Content Writer — whatever applies)
      • "Remote" and "Bangladesh" / "Dhaka" (location matters for timezone filtering)
      • Industry terms (e-commerce, SaaS, B2B, DTC)
      • Tool names (Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager, Salesforce, Figma — whatever you use)

      Sprinkle these naturally through your headline, about section, and experience bullets. Don't keyword-stuff — write for humans first, search second.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Should I use LinkedIn in English or Bangla?

      English. LinkedIn is a global professional platform, and recruiters hiring remotely search in English. Your profile should be 100% English.

      How many connections do I need?

      Quality over quantity, but aim for 500+ to unlock full search visibility (LinkedIn limits some features below 500). Connect with purpose — industry peers, potential clients, recruiters — not random strangers.

      Is LinkedIn Premium worth it for job seekers?

      The free version is enough to get started. Premium's main value is InMail (messaging people you're not connected to) and seeing who's viewed your profile. Try one month of Premium during an active job search, then cancel.

      What if I have no experience to show?

      Show potential instead: personal projects, certifications (Google Skillshop is free), volunteer work, and a well-written About section explaining what you're learning and where you're headed. Everyone starts somewhere — honesty about being early-career beats an empty profile.

      How often should I update my profile?

      Update it every time something changes: new role, new skill, new certification, new result.
