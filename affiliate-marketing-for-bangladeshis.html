Affiliate marketing means recommending products and earning a commission when someone buys through your link. No inventory, no customer service, no shipping. It sounds like the perfect online income — and it can be. But there's a catch most gurus skip: you need traffic before you earn commissions. No audience, no income. This guide shows you the realistic path, the programs that actually work for Bangladeshis, and the payment problems you must solve first.

How Affiliate Marketing Actually Works

You join a program (say, Daraz Affiliate). You get a unique link for a product. You recommend that product in your content — a YouTube review, a blog post, a Facebook post. Someone clicks your link and buys. The company tracks the purchase back to you and pays you a percentage, usually between 3% and 50% depending on the program. That's it. The company handles stock, payment, delivery, and support. You handle one thing: bringing buyers.

The Programs That Work for Bangladeshis

1. Daraz Affiliate Program (best for local audiences)

If your audience is Bangladeshi, start here. Daraz is the biggest e-commerce platform in the country, and its affiliate program pays commissions on products people actually buy — electronics, fashion, home goods. The advantage: your audience trusts Daraz, delivery is local, and payment is in taka to local banks or mobile wallets. Commissions are lower than software programs, but conversion is easier because the buying friction is low.

2. Amazon Associates (and the payment problem)

Amazon's program is famous and commissions are decent. But there's a serious problem for Bangladeshis: Amazon does not pay directly to Bangladeshi bank accounts. Your options are Amazon gift cards (not cash) or payment through a service like Payoneer, which gives you a virtual US bank account that Amazon can pay into. The Payoneer workaround does function — many Bangladeshi affiliates use it — but it adds fees and complexity. Go in with your eyes open.

3. Software and SaaS affiliate programs (highest commissions)

Web hosting companies, email tools, design tools, VPN services — many pay 30–50% recurring commissions. One hosting referral can pay you every month for years. These are the highest-earning affiliate programs, but they require an audience that buys software: freelancers, bloggers, marketers. Programs like Bluehost, Hostinger, ConvertKit, and Semrush all have affiliate programs open to international affiliates, and many pay via PayPal or Payoneer.

4. Local and regional programs

Beyond Daraz, look at other Bangladeshi platforms with affiliate or referral programs, Bangladeshi brands that pay for referrals, and regional marketplaces. These often have less competition than global programs.

The Content-First Approach (Non-Negotiable)

Here is the truth: affiliate marketing is a traffic business, not a link business. Dropping links in Facebook comments earns nothing and gets you marked as spam. You need a content channel that people trust. Pick one and commit:

  • YouTube: Review products, compare options, show real use. A "best budget headphones under 3,000 BDT" video with affiliate links in the description can earn for years. This is the strongest channel for affiliates in Bangladesh — video builds trust fast.
  • Blog: Write buying guides and comparison posts ("X vs Y"). Search traffic is passive and compounds. A review written once can generate commissions for years.
  • Facebook page or group: Build an audience around a niche (tech deals, fashion finds), share genuine recommendations. Works, but you're renting the audience — the algorithm can cut your reach anytime.

Don't try all three at once. Pick the one you can sustain for six months.

Step-by-Step: Your First 90 Days

Step 1: Pick a niche you actually use (Days 1–7).

Recommend products you know. Tech gadgets, budget fashion, books, software tools for freelancers — whatever you genuinely buy and understand. Never promote products you haven't researched. Your credibility is your only asset.

Step 2: Join 2–3 programs (Days 8–14).

Apply to the Daraz Affiliate Program plus one or two software programs that fit your niche. Approval usually takes a few days. Read each program's terms — some prohibit certain promotion methods.

Step 3: Create your content channel (Days 15–30).

Start the YouTube channel or blog. Publish your first 4–5 pieces of content. Focus on helping first: reviews, comparisons, "how to choose" guides. Place affiliate links naturally inside genuinely useful content.

Step 4: Publish consistently (Days 31–60).

Aim for 2–3 quality pieces per week. Study what gets views and clicks. Double down on what works. Learn basic SEO so your content ranks on Google and YouTube search.

Step 5: Track and optimize (Days 61–90).

Check which links get clicks and which content converts. Most affiliates find that 20% of their content generates 80% of commissions. Make more of what works. Cut what doesn't.

Realistic Timelines and Expectations

  • Month 1–3: Expect little to no income. You're building content and learning what your audience wants. Anyone promising faster results is selling you something.
  • Month 4–6: First commissions trickle in — often small. This is normal and actually a good sign; it means the system works.
  • Month 6–12: With consistent content (50+ quality pieces), commissions can become meaningful side income. The range varies enormously by niche and effort — there is no honest fixed number I can give you, and be suspicious of anyone who does.
  • Year 2+: Content compounds. Old reviews keep earning while new content adds more. This is where affiliate marketing's "passive" reputation comes from — but the first year is anything but passive.

Mistakes That Kill Beginners

  • Promoting too many products. Ten links to ten unrelated products earn less than one deep review of one product you believe in.
  • Ignoring disclosure. Tell your audience when links are affiliate links. It's required by most programs' terms, and honesty builds the trust that drives clicks.
  • Chasing high commissions over fit. A 50% commission on a product your audience doesn't want earns zero. A 5% commission on something they buy daily adds up.
  • Quitting at month two. Almost everyone quits right before the compounding starts. The winners are usually just the ones who published for six months straight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is affiliate marketing legal in Bangladesh?

Yes. Earning commissions from affiliate programs is legal. As income grows, declare it properly for tax purposes like any other income.

How do I receive affiliate payments in Bangladesh?

Daraz pays locally. International programs typically pay via PayPal or Payoneer. Payoneer lets you receive USD into a virtual account and withdraw to Bangladeshi banks — this is the standard workaround most Bangladeshi affiliates use.

Do I need a website to start affiliate marketing?

No, but it helps. You can start with just a YouTube channel or Facebook page. A blog gives you search traffic that compounds over time, so consider adding one as you grow.

How much can a beginner realistically earn?

Honestly, it varies too much for a fixed number. In the first 3–6 months, expect small amounts — enough to prove the model works. Meaningful side income typically takes 6–12 months of consistent content. Anyone guaranteeing specific earnings is not being honest with you.

Can I do affiliate marketing alongside a job?

Yes — it's one of the best fits for the after-office-hours model. Content creation (one video or article per week) fits around a full-time job, and old content keeps working while you're at the office.

Which is better: Daraz Affiliate or Amazon Associates?

For a Bangladeshi audience, Daraz — local trust, local delivery, local payments. For an international audience, Amazon or software programs pay more per sale but require Payoneer for payouts and an audience that buys in dollars. ## Final Thought Affiliate marketing rewards patience and punishes hype. Pick a niche, build a content channel, recommend products you genuinely believe in, and give it a full year. The people who win are not the ones with secret tricks — they're the ones who kept publishing when nothing was happening.
