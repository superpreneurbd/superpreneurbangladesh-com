A CV for remote jobs is different from a CV for local jobs. Local employers in Bangladesh often want your photo, your father's name, and your full educational history. Remote employers — especially international ones — want none of that. They want proof you can do the work, communicate clearly, and function without someone watching you.

This guide shows you exactly how to write one.

The Format

One page. Not two, not three. Remote hiring managers skim dozens of CVs; if yours needs scrolling, it loses.

Clean and simple. No fancy templates with graphics, no photos, no colorful headers. Use a standard font (Calibri, Arial, or Georgia), 10-11pt body text, clear section headings. Applicant tracking systems (ATS) can't read fancy designs — and many remote companies use them.

PDF, always. Never send a Word doc. Name it `Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf`, not `CV_final_FINAL_v3.pdf`.

Sections (In Order)

1. Header

Name, professional title, location, contact info. That's it.

```

Imtiaz Khan

Digital Marketing Specialist | Dhaka, Bangladesh (UTC+6)

imtiaz.khan@email.com | +880 1XXX-XXXXXX

linkedin.com/in/yourprofile | yourportfolio.com

```

Include: City and timezone. Remote employers need to know your overlap hours immediately.

Don't include: Photo, date of birth, marital status, religion, father's name, NID number. These are standard on Bangladeshi CVs but irrelevant (and sometimes illegal to ask about) internationally. Leave them off.

2. Professional Summary (3-4 Lines)

Not an objective ("Seeking a challenging position..."). A summary of what you offer.

Weak: "Hardworking digital marketer seeking remote opportunities to grow my career."

Strong: "Digital marketing specialist with 3 years managing Facebook and Google ads for e-commerce brands. Managed ৳40L+ in ad spend at 3.1x average ROAS. Experienced working async with UK and Australian clients across timezones."

The formula: role + years + proof + remote-relevant detail.

3. Skills

Two columns: technical skills and tools. Be specific.

Weak: "Digital marketing, social media, communication, MS Office"

Strong:

  • Paid ads: Meta Ads Manager, Google Ads, TikTok Ads
  • Analytics: Google Analytics 4, Meta Pixel, Looker Studio
  • Tools: Canva, Mailchimp, WordPress, Slack, Asana

Only list skills you can actually demonstrate. Interviewers will test them.

4. Experience

Reverse chronological. For each role: title, company, dates, then 3-5 bullet points.

The bullet formula: Action verb + what you did + measurable result.

Weak:

  • Responsible for managing the company's Facebook page
  • Worked on digital marketing campaigns
  • Helped with content creation

Strong:

  • Grew company Facebook page from 8K to 45K followers in 14 months through daily content and community engagement
  • Managed ৳12L annual ad budget across Facebook and Google; reduced cost-per-lead by 34% through audience testing
  • Wrote 60+ blog posts driving 25K monthly organic sessions (tracked in GA4)

For remote relevance, add:

  • "Worked fully remotely with a distributed team across 4 timezones"
  • "Managed async communication via Slack and Notion"
  • Any freelance or contract work (it proves self-management)

No formal experience? Include freelance projects, internships, volunteer work, and significant personal projects. Label them honestly: "Freelance Digital Marketer (Self-employed)" is perfectly legitimate.

5. Education

Degree, university, year. One line each. Unless you're a fresh graduate, education goes after experience — employers care about what you've done more than where you studied.

6. Portfolio / Links

Remote employers click links. Include:

  • Portfolio website
  • LinkedIn profile
  • GitHub (developers)
  • Writing samples (writers)
  • Case studies

Remote-Specific Additions That Win Interviews

Timezone Line

Under your location, add availability: "Available 2pm–10pm BDT (9am–5pm CET overlap)." This answers the employer's first timezone question before they ask it.

Remote Work Proof

If you've ever worked remotely — freelancing, contract work, even a remote internship — highlight it. Create a bullet: "Delivered all work remotely using async communication (Slack, email, Loom)."

If you haven't: your freelance history, personal projects, or even consistent content creation (a blog, a YouTube channel) proves you can work without supervision. Mention it.

Language Note

If your English is strong, say so plainly: "Professional working proficiency in written and spoken English." Don't oversell ("native-level" when it's not) — interviews will reveal the truth.

Mistakes Bangladeshi Applicants Make

  1. Including a photo. Standard in Bangladesh, but international employers don't expect it and some discard CVs with photos to avoid bias liability. Leave it off for international applications.
    1. Listing every job since 2015. Relevance beats completeness. If you worked retail for 2 years before switching to marketing, one line is enough (or drop it).
      1. No metrics. "Increased sales" means nothing. "Increased online sales 43% in 6 months" means everything.
        1. Generic skills section. "Hardworking, punctual, team player" — everyone writes this, nobody believes it. Show these traits through your achievements instead.
          1. Typos. One typo in a CV for a writing or marketing role is fatal. Read it aloud, use Grammarly, have a friend review it.
            1. Lying about experience. Remote employers verify. Fake client names, inflated titles, invented metrics — all discoverable in a 10-minute reference check. The truth with less experience beats lies with more.

              2. Tailoring for Each Application

              Don't send the same CV everywhere. For each application:

              • Mirror the job post's language (if they say "performance marketing," don't write "digital advertising")
              • Reorder bullets to put the most relevant achievements first
              • Adjust your professional summary's last line to reference the company or role

              This takes 15 minutes per application. It doubles your interview rate.

              Frequently Asked Questions

              Should my CV be in English or Bangla?

              English for all remote and international applications. Bangla only if the job post specifically requests it (rare for remote roles).

              What if I have a career gap?

              Be honest and brief. "2023–2024: Freelance projects and skills development" is fine. Gaps for family, health, or study are normal — don't hide them, don't over-explain them.

              Can I use a CV template from the internet?

              Yes, but pick a simple ATS-friendly one. Avoid templates with graphics, tables, text boxes, or multiple columns — parsing software mangles them. When in doubt, plain formatting wins.

              How do I show freelance work on a CV?

              As a regular job entry: "Freelance Digital Marketing Consultant | Self-employed | 2022–Present" followed by client results (anonymized if needed: "E-commerce client, UK"). Freelance experience counts — don't hide it at the bottom.

              Should I include references?

              "References available on request" is enough.
