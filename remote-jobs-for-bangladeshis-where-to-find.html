A remote job is different from freelancing. Instead of juggling clients, you work for one company — with a fixed salary, regular hours, and (usually) benefits — but from home in Bangladesh. For many people, it's the best of both worlds: stable income without the Dhaka commute.

The challenge is knowing where to look. Most global job boards are flooded with applicants, and many "remote" listings secretly require US or EU residency. This guide lists the platforms where Bangladeshis actually get hired, and how to apply so you stand out.

International Remote Job Boards

1. We Work Remotely

One of the largest remote-only job boards. Categories include programming, marketing, customer support, and design. Competition is high, but the listings are genuinely remote — no location tricks.

Tip for Bangladeshis: Filter by "Customer Support" and "Marketing" — these categories hire internationally more often than engineering roles that sometimes require specific timezones.

2. RemoteOK

A massive aggregator of remote jobs, especially in tech. The site tags jobs by timezone requirements, so you can filter for roles that accept UTC+6 overlap.

Tip: Set up email alerts for your skill keywords. Good remote jobs get hundreds of applications within hours — speed matters.

3. FlexJobs

A curated, scam-free job board (paid subscription, but it filters out junk). Worth it if you're serious — the time you save not sifting through scams pays for itself.

4. AngelList (Wellfound)

Startup jobs, many remote-friendly. Startups are more open to hiring from Bangladesh than big corporations because they care about output, not office politics.

5. Turing

Turing specifically hires developers from around the world — including Bangladesh — for US companies. The vetting process is rigorous (coding tests and interviews), but passing it means access to high-paying long-term roles.

6. Toptal

Elite freelance network that functions like remote employment for top performers. Only about 3% of applicants pass the screening, but those who do get premium clients. Worth attempting once you have 2-3 years of experience.

Freelance Platforms That Lead to Remote Jobs

7. Upwork

Many "freelance" contracts on Upwork turn into long-term remote positions. Clients who like your work often offer ongoing roles. Treat every contract as a job interview — reliability gets you converted.

8. Contra

A newer platform popular with designers and creatives. The portfolio-first approach suits Bangladeshis with strong visual work but thin resumes.

Bangladesh-Focused Platforms

9. Bdjobs.com

The largest job portal in Bangladesh. It has a growing "work from home" filter. Many local companies now hire remotely — especially for digital marketing, content, and customer support.

10. LinkedIn Jobs

Use LinkedIn's remote filter with location set to Bangladesh. Many multinational companies with Dhaka offices also list remote-friendly roles. An optimized LinkedIn profile is essential here — see our LinkedIn optimization guide.

11. Facebook Groups

Groups like "Remote Jobs for Bangladeshis" and digital marketing communities post openings daily. Less formal, faster hiring, but verify every opportunity — scams exist here too.

Niche Boards Worth Checking

12. Working Nomads

Curated remote jobs, strong in marketing and project management.

13. Remotive

Community-driven remote job board with a Slack group. Good for support and operations roles.

14. Jobspresso

Hand-picked remote jobs in tech, marketing, and customer support.

15. Dynamite Jobs

Focuses on remote startups. Smaller pool, but less competition per listing.

What Remote Employers Look For

After reviewing hundreds of remote job posts, the pattern is clear. Employers hiring from Bangladesh care about:

  1. Written English. Most remote communication is text — Slack, email, docs. If your written English is clear, you're ahead of half the applicants.
  2. Timezone overlap. Bangladesh (UTC+6) overlaps with the European working day. Mention your availability explicitly: "Available 2pm-10pm BDT (9am-5pm CET overlap)."
  3. Self-management proof. Remote employers fear hiring someone who disappears. Show evidence: freelance history, personal projects, consistent content — anything that proves you work without supervision.
  4. Specific skills, not generic claims. "Digital marketer" is vague. "Facebook ads specialist for e-commerce, 3 years, managed ৳50L+ ad spend" gets interviews.

    5. How to Apply So You Get Replies

    • Customize every application. Generic applications get deleted. Reference something specific about the company in your first two sentences.
    • Lead with results, not duties. "Grew client's Instagram from 2K to 15K followers in 6 months" beats "Responsible for social media management."
    • Include a short video intro. A 60-second Loom video introducing yourself makes you memorable. Almost no Bangladeshi applicants do this — which is exactly why it works.
    • Follow up once. If no reply in a week, send one polite follow-up. Then move on.

    Red Flags: Remote Job Scams

    • Asking for money upfront (fees, deposits, "training costs")
    • Interviews only via text chat with no video call
    • Offers that are far above market rate for the role
    • Requests for bank login details or OTPs
    • Companies with no website, no LinkedIn presence, no verifiable identity

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can Bangladeshis legally work remotely for foreign companies?

    Yes. Many Bangladeshis work as contractors for foreign companies. You'll typically be hired as an independent contractor, invoice monthly, and receive payment via Payoneer or Wise. Keep records for tax purposes — see our [freelancer tax guide](tax-guide-for-freelancers-bangladesh.html).

    What salary can I expect from a remote job?

    It varies widely by role and experience. Entry-level remote support or VA roles often pay more than equivalent Dhaka office jobs. Experienced developers and marketers working for US/EU companies can earn several times the local market rate. The key variable is always skill level and proven results.

    Do I need perfect English for remote jobs?

    No — you need clear, professional written English. Most remote work is async (Slack, email, documents), so you don't need a perfect accent. Focus on writing clearly and concisely.

    How do timezones work with US companies?

    It's the hardest part. US overlap means working evenings/nights in Bangladesh. Many Bangladeshis prefer EU/UK/Australian companies for saner hours. Be honest with yourself about what schedule you can sustain.

    Should I quit my local job to go remote?

    Not immediately.
