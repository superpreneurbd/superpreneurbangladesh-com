Email marketing means sending useful emails to people who asked to hear from you. You collect their email addresses, then send them tips, offers, and updates. It is one of the oldest online marketing methods — and it still works.

I am Imtiaz Khan, a Dhaka-based digital marketer, and this guide covers exactly how to start: free tools, building your list from zero, what to write, and selling email as a service.

Why Email Still Works: You Own the List

Here is the biggest reason email beats social media: you own your email list. Nobody can take it from you.

Think about it. Your Facebook page has 10,000 followers, but Facebook decides how many of them see your post. On a bad day, it might be 200 people. Your reach depends on an algorithm you do not control.

Your email list is different. When you send an email to 1,000 subscribers, it lands in 1,000 inboxes. No algorithm decides. No platform can ban your audience overnight. It is the only audience you truly own.

Email also converts better than social posts. A subscriber already trusts you enough to share their email — that trust turns into sales more easily than a casual Facebook like.

The math is simple: social followers are rented, email subscribers are owned. Build on land you own.

An Honest Note on Bangladesh's Email Culture

Let me be honest with you. Email culture in Bangladesh is weaker than in the US or UK.

Many Bangladeshis rarely check email — they live on Facebook, Messenger, and WhatsApp. So your open rates will likely be lower than global averages. That is normal. Do not panic.

But here is the other side: each subscriber is worth more. The people who do open emails in Bangladesh tend to be professionals, business owners, and serious buyers — exactly the people with money to spend. A list of 500 engaged Bangladeshi subscribers can outperform 5,000 casual Facebook followers.

Also, email faces less competition here. Most Bangladeshi businesses never send emails at all. Their customers' inboxes are empty. When you show up with something useful, you stand out immediately.

So the honest picture is: smaller lists, lower open rates, but higher value per subscriber and almost no competition. That is a good deal.

Free Tools to Start With

You do not need to pay anything to start. These tools all have free plans that are enough for a beginner:

  • Mailchimp — The most well-known option with a solid free plan for small lists. Easy to learn.
  • Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) — Generous free plan. Good if your list grows fast.
  • Sender — Simple and lightweight. Good for beginners.

Pick one and stick with it. Do not spend a week comparing tools — they all do the same basic job at the start. What matters is that you start collecting emails and sending them.

One important rule: never send marketing emails from your personal Gmail. Gmail is not built for bulk sending, and you can get your account flagged. Always use a proper email marketing tool. It also gives you unsubscribe links and open tracking, which Gmail cannot do.

Building a List From Zero

Nobody hands you their email for nothing. Offer something valuable in return — called a lead magnet.

Step 1: Create a Simple Lead Magnet

A lead magnet is a free gift people get when they join your list. Keep it simple and useful:

  • A one-page checklist ("10-point Fiverr profile checklist")
  • A short PDF guide ("5 online income ideas for students")
  • A free video lesson or template
  • A discount code (for shops: "10% off your first order")

The best lead magnets solve one small problem fast. Do not create a 50-page ebook. Create something people can use in ten minutes.

Step 2: Put Signup Forms Everywhere

Add your signup form in every place your audience already finds you:

  • Your website: a form in the sidebar, at the end of articles, and one popup per visit.
  • Your Facebook page: pin a post with the signup link, add it to your page's action button.
  • Your YouTube videos: mention the free gift and put the link in the description and pinned comment.
  • At checkout: if you sell products, add a checkbox at checkout — "Send me offers and updates." Most buyers will tick it.

Step 3: Promote the Lead Magnet, Not the Newsletter

Nobody wants to "join a newsletter." Everyone wants the free checklist. Promote the gift, and signups happen naturally.

Welcome Email Sequence Basics (3 to 5 Emails)

When someone joins your list, do not go silent. Send a welcome sequence — automatic emails that build the relationship. Here is a 4-email structure:

Email 1 — Deliver the gift (send immediately). Subject: "Here is your [checklist/guide]." Give the download link, say welcome, and tell them what to expect.

Email 2 — Your story (day 2). Subject: "Why I started [your topic]." Share who you are and why you do this. People connect with people, not brands.

Email 3 — Quick win (day 4). Subject: "Try this today: [one simple tip]." Give one actionable tip. This proves your emails are worth opening.

Email 4 — Soft offer (day 7). Subject: "Want help with [their problem]?" Introduce your product or service gently — explain how you help and invite a reply.

Set this up once as an automation. Every new subscriber gets the same warm welcome while you sleep.

Writing Subject Lines That Get Opened

The subject line decides whether your email gets opened or ignored. Follow these rules:

  • Keep it short. Under 50 characters. Long subjects get cut off on phones.
  • Be specific. "3 Fiverr mistakes costing you clients" beats "Important update."
  • Create curiosity, not clickbait. Tease the benefit honestly. "The pricing trick I wish I knew earlier" works. "You won't BELIEVE this!!!" does not.
  • Use the reader's language. Write like you talk. "How I got my first client" beats "A Comprehensive Analysis of Client Acquisition Methodologies."
  • Test one thing at a time. Send half your list subject A and half subject B, then keep the winner.

And the golden rule: the subject line must match the email inside. Trick people once and they will never open your emails again. Trust is the whole game.

The Basic Rules: Permission, Unsubscribe, No Spam

Email marketing has rules. Break them and you destroy your reputation — or get banned.

  1. Only email people who said yes. Never buy email lists or add random addresses. Every subscriber must opt in through your form or checkout — this is called permission, and it is non-negotiable.
  2. Always include an unsubscribe link. Every email tool adds this automatically. People who want to leave should leave easily.
  3. Never spam. Spam means sending emails people did not ask for, or sending too many. One to four emails per month is safe for most businesses.
  4. Send from a real person. Use a name like "Imtiaz from Superpreneur," not a faceless "noreply@" address. And actually read the replies — your best customer conversations happen there.

    5. Follow these four rules and you will stay out of spam folders and in people's good books.

    Simple Campaign Ideas for F-Commerce and Ecommerce Sellers

    If you sell products online, email gives you direct access to buyers. Try these:

    • Abandoned cart reminder: "You left something in your cart." Send it 24 hours after an unfinished checkout. This one email recovers lost sales on autopilot.
    • New arrival announcement: "New collection just dropped." Your list hears first. Early buyers love feeling special.
    • Festival and sale campaigns: Eid, Puja, Pohela Boishakh, 11.11 — plan one email per big occasion with a real offer.
    • Restock alert: "Back in stock: [popular product]." It sells fast because the demand is already there.
    • Customer win-back: "We miss you — here is 10% off." Send to buyers quiet for 90 days. Winning back an old customer is cheaper than finding a new one.

    Start with one campaign type. Master it. Then add the next. You do not need all five on day one.

    Offer Email Marketing as a Service

    Almost no small business in Bangladesh does email marketing. That means almost no competition for you as a provider.

    A simple starter package looks like this:

    • Set up the email tool and signup forms for the client
    • Create one lead magnet (checklist or discount offer)
    • Write a 4-email welcome sequence
    • Send 2 campaigns per month (new arrivals, festival offers)
    • Send a simple monthly report (subscribers gained, open rate, sales from email)

    Learn on your own list first — even a tiny one. Your welcome sequence and campaigns become your portfolio. Then approach clothing brands, cosmetics sellers, and course creators on Facebook with a one-month trial offer.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is email marketing dead in 2026?

    No. Personal, useful emails to people who opted in work better than ever — especially in Bangladesh, where few businesses use email at all.

    How many subscribers do I need before email is worth it?

    Start from subscriber one. A list of 100 engaged subscribers beats 10,000 cold ones. Set up your welcome sequence now so every new subscriber gets value from day one.

    Which free email tool is best for beginners in Bangladesh?

    Mailchimp, Brevo, and Sender all have solid free plans — pick whichever you find easiest. Never send bulk email from personal Gmail.

    How often should I send marketing emails?

    For most businesses, 2 to 4 emails per month is the sweet spot. If unsubscribes spike after an email, you sent too often or the content missed.

    Can I really sell email marketing as a service with no experience?

    Yes, but build proof first. Run email for your own project for 60 to 90 days, then screenshot your welcome sequence, open rates, and sales.
