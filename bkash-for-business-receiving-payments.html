Almost every online transaction in Bangladesh touches bKash. If you sell digital products, run a Facebook shop, take freelance payments from local clients, or collect course fees, bKash is where your customers expect to pay.

But there is a right way and a wrong way to use it for business. This guide covers how to receive payments professionally — whether you are just starting or already taking regular orders.

Personal Account vs Merchant Account: What Is the Difference?

Most people start with a personal bKash account — the one linked to your NID that you use for everyday transfers. For a brand-new side business with a handful of payments a month, a personal account technically works.

But as volume grows, a personal account creates problems:

  • Transaction limits. Personal accounts have daily and monthly limits on how much you can receive and send. A busy sales week can hit the ceiling fast.
  • No business features. No payment links, no QR stand, no settlement reports — the tools that make collecting money from many customers manageable.
  • Mixed money. Business income and personal spending in one account makes bookkeeping a nightmare and tax filing harder.
  • Trust. Customers paying a business expect a business payment experience, not a "send money to my personal number" message.

A bKash merchant account (often called a business or payment account) is designed for receiving customer payments at scale, with higher limits, QR-based collection, and proper transaction records.

Rule of thumb: starting out with occasional payments? Personal is fine temporarily. Taking regular payments from customers? Move to a merchant setup.

How Customers Pay a Business on bKash

Understanding the customer side helps you set things up properly. Customers can pay you through:

  1. Send Money — the customer sends from their bKash to yours. Simple but manual; you must confirm each payment yourself.
  2. Payment to merchant — the customer uses the "Payment" option to a merchant number or QR. This is the professional route for businesses.
  3. QR code scan — you display a QR code (at a shop, on a flyer, or as an image online), and the customer scans and pays. Fast and reduces wrong-number errors.
  4. Payment links — for online sellers, a link the customer clicks to pay a fixed amount. Useful for digital products and course sales.

    5. For online businesses in Bangladesh, QR codes and payment links do the heavy lifting — they eliminate the "wrong number" problem that plagues manual Send Money payments.

    Setting Up: The Practical Path

    If you are just starting (testing the waters)

    1. Use your existing personal bKash account temporarily.
    2. Create a simple payment instruction for customers: your bKash number, the exact amount, and ask them to send the transaction ID (TrxID) after paying.
    3. Log every payment in a spreadsheet: date, customer name, amount, TrxID. This takes two minutes per sale and saves hours later.
    4. Set a deadline for yourself: once you cross a consistent monthly volume, upgrade to a merchant setup.

      5. If you are taking regular payments (the professional setup)

      1. Register a merchant account through bKash's business onboarding — this typically requires your trade license or business documentation and your NID. Requirements change, so check bKash's current business registration page for the exact list.
      2. Get your merchant number and QR code. Display the QR wherever customers pay — your Facebook page, your website checkout, your invoices.
      3. Set up transaction notifications. Make sure you get an SMS or app notification for every incoming payment so you can confirm orders in real time.
      4. Reconcile daily. Match incoming payments against your orders. Unmatched payments usually mean a customer forgot to include their order reference.

        5. The TrxID System: Your Best Friend

        For manual payments, the transaction ID (TrxID) is everything. Every bKash transaction generates one, and it is the only reliable proof that a specific payment happened.

        Make it a rule: no order is confirmed until you have the TrxID and have verified the amount in your own bKash app. Screenshots can be faked; the TrxID in your own transaction history cannot.

        Ask customers to send the TrxID in a fixed format, for example: "Order #1234 — TrxID 9HX7K2L4M5 — ৳1,500." This makes reconciliation dramatically easier when you have dozens of orders.

        Pricing Your Products With bKash in Mind

        When you set prices, remember that bKash cash-out has a cost on someone's side — either you or the customer pays it. Decide your policy upfront and state it clearly:

        • "bKash charge included" — you absorb the cash-out cost in your price. Simpler for customers, slightly lower margin for you.
        • "Send exact amount; cash-out charge on us" — same idea, stated differently.
        • "Customer bears the charge" — you ask customers to add the fee. Common, but adds friction.

        Whatever you choose, put it in writing on your order page or checkout instructions. Surprise charges are the fastest way to lose a customer in Bangladesh.

        Keeping Business Money Separate

        This is the advice most small sellers ignore — and regret later:

        1. Use a dedicated bKash number for business if possible, separate from your personal one. A second SIM is cheap; the clarity is priceless.
        2. Transfer business earnings to your bank regularly. Do not let large balances sit in a mobile wallet indefinitely.
        3. Keep a simple ledger. Date, customer, product, amount received, TrxID. A Google Sheet is enough. This is also what makes tax filing possible later.
        4. Separate business expenses too. When you pay for ads, tools, or delivery from business money, log it. Profit is revenue minus expenses — you cannot know your profit if you do not track both.

          5. Common Problems (And Fixes)

          "Customer sent to the wrong number." This happens constantly with manual Send Money. Fix: switch to QR codes or payment links, which remove the typing step.

          "Customer claims they paid but I see nothing." Ask for the TrxID, then check your own transaction history. If the TrxID does not appear in your history, the payment did not reach you — the customer may have sent it to a wrong number.

          "I hit my receiving limit mid-campaign." Personal account limits are real. If you run a promotion, warn customers in advance — or better, upgrade to a merchant account before the campaign.

          "Too many payments to track manually." That is the signal to move to merchant tools with proper reporting. Manual tracking breaks somewhere around 20–30 transactions a day.

          bKash for Freelancers With Local Clients

          Freelancers usually receive international payments via Payoneer or Wise — but local clients pay in BDT, and they pay via bKash. For local client work:

          • Invoice in BDT with your bKash number and a payment deadline.
          • Ask for the TrxID on payment.
          • For retainers, set a fixed monthly payment date so you are not chasing money.

          Frequently Asked Questions

          Can I use my personal bKash account for business?

          For very small, occasional sales, yes — but it is not ideal. Limits, mixed finances, and unprofessional payment experience become problems quickly. Upgrade to a merchant setup once sales are regular.

          What documents do I need for a bKash merchant account?

          Requirements change over time, but typically include your NID and business documentation such as a trade license. Check bKash's official business page for the current list before applying.

          How do I confirm a customer really paid?

          Check your own bKash transaction history for the TrxID the customer provides. The payment must appear in YOUR history — a screenshot from the customer is not proof.

          Should I absorb the bKash cash-out charge or pass it to customers?

          Either works, but decide upfront and state it clearly. Absorbing it (pricing it in) gives customers a smoother experience. Passing it on is common but adds friction at checkout.

          What is the safest way to collect payments online?

          QR codes and payment links beat manual "send money to this number" — they eliminate wrong-number errors and make the amount fixed. For manual payments, always verify the TrxID in your own app.

          How do I keep records for taxes?

          Log every payment: date, customer, amount, TrxID. Transfer business earnings to your bank regularly.
