New freelancers often ask: "What tools do I need?" Then they sign up for 15 apps, get overwhelmed, and spend more time managing tools than doing work.

The truth: you need about 10 tools. Everything else is optional until you are earning consistently.

This guide covers the essential toolkit for Bangladeshi freelancers — what you need, what is nice to have, and what to skip until later.

Category 1: Communication

WhatsApp Business (Free)

Your primary client communication tool in Bangladesh. Most local clients prefer WhatsApp over email.

Why the Business version: Separate business profile, automated greeting messages, quick replies for common questions, labels to organize chats. It looks professional and keeps business separate from personal chats.

Zoom or Google Meet (Free tiers available)

For video calls with international clients. Google Meet is simpler (no download needed, works in browser). Zoom has better features for longer calls.

Tip: Always test your audio/video 10 minutes before client calls. "Can you hear me?" wastes everyone's time.

Email (Free — Gmail)

You need a professional email address. `yourname@gmail.com` works fine when starting. As you grow, consider `you@yourdomain.com` for credibility.

Do not: Use unprofessional addresses (coolguy1998@gmail.com). Your email is part of your brand.

Category 2: Getting Paid

Payoneer (Free to set up)

The standard for Bangladeshi freelancers receiving international payments. Connects to Upwork, Fiverr, and direct client transfers. Withdraw to Bangladeshi bank accounts.

Our detailed Payoneer setup guide walks through the process. Payoneer vs Wise comparison helps you choose.

bKash / Nagad (Free)

Essential for local clients. Most Bangladeshi businesses pay via bKash or Nagad. Have both set up with merchant-friendly configurations.

Setting up bKash for business and Nagad merchant accounts are covered in detail.

Wave or Google Sheets (Free)

For invoicing and tracking income. Wave is free invoicing software. Google Sheets works fine for simple tracking.

Track from day one: Every payment received, every expense. Our invoicing guide covers professional invoicing.

Category 3: Finding Work

Upwork and Fiverr (Free to join)

The two biggest freelance platforms. Our comparison helps you choose where to start. Best freelance websites for Bangladeshis covers alternatives.

LinkedIn (Free)

Not just for job seekers. LinkedIn is where high-value clients find freelancers. A complete profile with portfolio samples attracts inbound inquiries.

Optimize your LinkedIn profile before you start outreach.

Category 4: Doing the Work

Google Workspace (Free)

Docs, Sheets, Drive — free and sufficient for most freelance work. Real-time collaboration, automatic saving, accessible from anywhere.

Upgrade to paid only when you need custom email domains or more storage.

Canva (Free tier is generous)

For quick graphics, social media posts, presentations, and simple designs. The free tier covers most freelancer needs.

Our Canva-to-client guide shows how to turn Canva skills into income.

CapCut (Free)

For video editing. Surprisingly powerful for a free tool. Good enough for client video projects, social media content, and YouTube videos.

Video editing with CapCut covers the path from beginner to paid work.

Notion or Google Keep (Free)

For organizing projects, notes, and ideas. Google Keep is simpler. Notion is more powerful. Pick one — do not use both.

Category 5: AI Tools (The 2026 Advantage)

AI tools are not optional anymore — they are how you compete.

ChatGPT or Claude (Free tiers available)

For drafting, brainstorming, editing, research summaries, and overcoming blank-page syndrome. Use AI as an assistant, not a replacement — clients pay for your judgment, not raw output.

Important: Never submit raw AI output as client work. Use AI for first drafts and ideas, then apply your expertise.

Grammarly (Free tier works)

For non-native English speakers, Grammarly catches errors that undermine professionalism. The free tier is sufficient for most needs.

Perplexity (Free tier available)

For research. Better than Google for getting sourced, summarized answers quickly.

Category 6: Productivity

Google Calendar (Free)

For scheduling deep work blocks, client calls, and deadlines. Our time management system depends on calendar blocking.

Todoist or Google Tasks (Free)

For task management. Keep it simple — one list for today, one for this week.

A Timer (Free — your phone)

For deep work sessions and the Pomodoro technique. No fancy app needed.

Category 7: Backup and Security

Google Drive or Dropbox (Free tiers)

Back up all client work. Losing a client's project to a dead laptop is a career-ending event. Automatic cloud backup is non-negotiable.

Rule: If it is not backed up in two places, it does not exist.

A Password Manager (Free — Bitwarden)

You will accumulate dozens of logins (client accounts, platforms, tools). Reusing passwords is dangerous. Bitwarden is free and excellent.

2FA Authenticator App (Free — Google Authenticator or Authy)

Enable two-factor authentication on every important account. Your freelance accounts are your livelihood — protect them.

What You Do NOT Need (Yet)

Do not buy these until you are earning consistently:

  • Expensive project management software (Asana paid, Monday.com) — Google Sheets works fine initially
  • Premium stock photo subscriptions — free alternatives (Unsplash, Pexels) are sufficient
  • A fancy website with paid hosting — start with free portfolio options
  • Multiple AI subscriptions — one free-tier AI tool is enough to start
  • Paid courses on "freelancing" — free resources (including this site and our YouTube channel) cover the fundamentals

The rule: Every tool should either save you time or make you money. If it does neither, cancel it.

The Starter Setup (Total Cost: Nearly Free)

If you are starting today with minimal budget, here is your setup:

  1. Gmail (free) — email
  2. WhatsApp Business (free) — client chat
  3. Google Meet (free) — video calls
  4. Payoneer (free) — international payments
  5. bKash/Nagad (free) — local payments
  6. Google Docs/Sheets/Drive (free) — work and backup
  7. Canva free — graphics
  8. CapCut free — video
  9. ChatGPT free — AI assistance
  10. Google Calendar + phone timer — productivity

    11. Total monthly cost: 0 taka. You can run a real freelance business on free tools. Upgrade when revenue justifies it, not before.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Should I pay for Upwork Connects?

    Upwork's free Connects are limited. If you are actively bidding, buying a small Connects pack is reasonable — it is a business investment with direct ROI. But fix your profile and proposals first; paid Connects with a weak profile wastes money.

    Is it safe to use free AI tools for client work?

    Yes, with caveats. Do not input confidential client data into free AI tools (check privacy policies). Always review and edit AI output — never submit it raw. Disclose AI assistance if your client agreement requires it.

    What about a VPN? Do freelancers need one?

    A VPN is useful for accessing geo-restricted tools and for security on public WiFi. It is not essential for most freelancers. If you handle sensitive client data or travel frequently, consider one.

    How do I manage subscriptions as they grow?

    Review quarterly. List every paid tool, its monthly cost, and what it earns or saves you. Cancel anything that does not justify its cost. Subscription creep is real — 500 taka here, 1000 there adds up.

    What is the single most important tool investment?

    A UPS for your internet router during load-shedding season. No software matters if you cannot get online.
