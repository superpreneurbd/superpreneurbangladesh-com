Getting the work done is only half the job. Getting paid — on time, in full, without awkward follow-ups — is the other half. And it starts with a professional invoice.

Many Bangladeshi freelancers either skip invoicing entirely (relying on chat messages like "please send $200") or send sloppy invoices that make them look amateur. Both cost money. This guide shows you how to invoice international clients properly.

Why Invoicing Matters (Even for Small Jobs)

An invoice is not just a payment request — it is a business document that:

  • Sets clear expectations. Amount, due date, and payment method in writing — no "I thought it was $150" misunderstandings.
  • Makes you look professional. Clients pay professional-looking freelancers faster. It is human nature.
  • Creates a paper trail. If a payment dispute ever arises, your invoice is your evidence.
  • Simplifies your bookkeeping. Every invoice is a record of income earned — essential for tax filing.

On Fiverr and Upwork, the platform handles invoicing for you. This guide is for direct clients — the ones you find yourself, who pay you via Payoneer, Wise, or bank transfer.

What Every Invoice Must Include

A proper invoice has these elements — no exceptions:

  1. The word "INVOICE" at the top, with a unique invoice number (e.g., INV-2026-001). Number sequentially; it keeps your records clean.
  2. Your details: full name, email, address, and phone. This is your business identity.
  3. The client's details: name (or company name), email, and address.
  4. Invoice date and payment due date. Standard terms are 7, 14, or 30 days — state it explicitly ("Payment due within 14 days").
  5. Line items: what you did, in plain language. "Blog post — 1,500 words — $80." Vague lines like "services rendered" invite disputes.
  6. Total amount and currency (USD, EUR, etc.). Never leave currency ambiguous.
  7. Payment instructions: exactly how to pay you — your Payoneer receiving account details, Wise details, or whichever method you agreed on.
  8. Late payment note (optional but smart): "A 1.5% monthly late fee applies after the due date." You may never enforce it, but it signals seriousness.

    9. Invoice Tools: Free Options That Work

    You do not need paid software. These free options handle invoicing well:

    • Wave — free invoicing and accounting, popular with freelancers worldwide.
    • Zoho Invoice — free tier is generous for solo freelancers.
    • PayPal invoicing — useful if the client pays via PayPal (note: PayPal receiving is limited in Bangladesh, so this is mainly for sending professional invoices, not receiving through PayPal).
    • Google Docs/Sheets templates — a clean template you reuse works fine when starting out. Save each invoice as a PDF with the invoice number in the filename.
    • Wise — if you use Wise, its invoicing features integrate with receiving payments.

    Pick one tool and stick with it. Switching tools every month scatters your records.

    Setting Payment Terms

    Payment terms are the rules of the transaction. Agree on them before starting work — not after delivery when your leverage is gone.

    For new clients:

    • 50% upfront, 50% on delivery is the standard for project work. It filters out non-serious clients instantly.
    • For small jobs under $100, full upfront payment is reasonable.
    • For hourly work, weekly or bi-weekly invoicing keeps cash flow steady.

    For ongoing/retainer clients:

    • Monthly invoicing on a fixed date (e.g., the 1st of each month).
    • Payment due within 7–14 days of the invoice.

    Put terms in writing — in your proposal, contract, or at minimum in an email the client confirms. "As discussed, payment terms are 50% upfront ($250) and 50% on delivery, due within 7 days of invoice." That one sentence prevents most payment problems.

    The Invoicing Workflow (Step by Step)

    1. Agree on scope and price before work begins. Confirm in writing.
    2. Do the work. Keep the client updated — surprises at invoice time usually mean surprises during the project.
    3. Generate the invoice the day you deliver (not a week later). Same-day invoicing gets paid faster.
    4. Send it by email with a short, polite note: "Hi [Name], attached is invoice INV-2026-014 for $400, due November 1. Payment details are on the invoice. Thanks!"
    5. Log it in your tracker: invoice number, client, amount, date sent, due date, status.
    6. Follow up if unpaid (see below).

      7. Chasing Late Payments (Without Burning Bridges)

      Late payments happen. Handle them with a system, not with anxiety:

      • Day 1 after due date: friendly reminder. "Hi [Name], just checking — invoice INV-2026-014 ($400) was due yesterday. Let me know if you need anything from my side to process it."
      • Day 7: firmer follow-up. "Following up on invoice INV-2026-014, now 7 days overdue. Please advise on payment timing."
      • Day 14: direct. "Invoice INV-2026-014 is now 14 days overdue. I need payment processed this week to continue our working relationship."
      • Day 30+: final notice mentioning you will pause all work until payment clears — and consider whether this client is worth keeping.

      Key principles:

      • Stay polite but firm. You are a professional collecting what you earned, not begging.
      • Never do new work for a client with an overdue invoice. "Happy to start the next phase as soon as the outstanding invoice is settled."
      • Document everything in email, not just chat. Chat messages get lost; emails are records.

      For persistent non-payment, our guide on handling payment disputes covers escalation steps.

      Invoicing in USD While Living on BDT

      You invoice in USD (or EUR/GBP), but you spend in BDT. A few practical notes:

      • State the currency on every invoice. "USD 400" — never just "400."
      • The client pays the invoiced currency. Currency conversion happens on your side when you withdraw via Payoneer or Wise — do not invoice in BDT for international clients unless they specifically request it.
      • Watch the exchange rate when planning your income, but do not try to time conversions obsessively. A regular withdrawal rhythm beats market-timing.
      • Keep invoices and receipts matched. When the USD 400 invoice becomes BDT in your bank, log both figures. Your bookkeeping should show the full chain.

      Common Invoicing Mistakes

      • No invoice at all. "Just send the money" works until it does not — then you have zero documentation.
      • Invoicing late. Every day you delay invoicing is a day you delay payment. Invoice on delivery day.
      • Vague line items. "Services" means nothing. Describe the deliverable.
      • No due date. Without one, "later" stretches forever.
      • No payment instructions. Make paying you effortless — the exact account details, right on the invoice.
      • Mixing currencies. One invoice, one currency, stated clearly.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Do I need to invoice clients on Fiverr and Upwork?

      No — the platforms handle billing, receipts, and payment protection for you. Invoicing matters for direct clients outside the platforms.

      What currency should I invoice in?

      The currency you agreed with the client — usually USD for international clients. State it explicitly on every invoice.

      How do I number my invoices?

      Sequentially: INV-2026-001, INV-2026-002, and so on. Never reuse numbers, never skip. It keeps your records clean and looks professional.

      What if a client refuses to pay upfront?

      For new clients, 50% upfront is standard and non-negotiable for project work. A client who refuses any upfront payment is a red flag — proceed only if you are willing to risk the full amount.

      Can I charge a late fee?

      Yes, if you stated it on the invoice or in your agreed terms. Even if you never enforce it, the clause itself encourages on-time payment.

      How long should I keep invoices?

      Keep them for several years — at minimum, long enough to cover your country's tax record requirements. Digital copies (PDFs) in an organized folder are fine.
